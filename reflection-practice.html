Reflection Practice

The starting point of the cycle — and the theme of this whole site. Know where you are before you decide what to change.

Core practice The Framework Try It

Why It Matters

Reflecting is a way to boost critical thinking and self-awareness. You can't course-correct what you never looked at. A quick check-in — daily, weekly, monthly, or quarterly, or honestly all of the above at different scales — is what catches a problem early enough to actually fix it, instead of noticing the same mistake months later, after it's already happened a dozen times. Reflection isn't the reward for finishing Plan and Do; it's the step that makes the next round of both worth running.

This is also the stage most tightly coupled to Plan — not a separate loop, the same one. What you notice here doesn't stay here: End-of-Month Wins tell you whether last month's SMART goals or OKRs actually held up, and Core Goals / Growth Goals become the raw material for next month's Goal Setting & Priorities. Reflect without feeding it back into Plan, and it's just journaling.

How to Reflect

  • Write it down right away — jot down details while the event is still fresh in your mind.
  • Use guiding questions — ask yourself what happened, how you felt, and what you'd do differently next time.

Tools & Systems

The practice matters more than the tool — Notion, plain paper, Bullet Journaling, or any other system runs the same loop just as well. Four cadences, all variations on the same handful of prompts:

Daily Reflection End of Week Monthly Log Quarterly Review SBI STARR
1

Daily Reflection

A two-minute end-of-day check-in, four prompts:

  • Goals & Progress — what were you aiming for today, and what did you actually complete?
  • What Went Well — what was the best part of today?
  • What Got in the Way — what could have been better, and what's still frustrating you?

Tomorrow — what's one thing that would make tomorrow better? Carry it straight into Prioritize Work.

2

End of Week

A shorter cadence between Daily and Monthly. Four prompts:

  • Wins — what got done this week that you're genuinely happy with?
  • Carryover — what's still open, and should it move to next week or get dropped?
  • Patterns — anything that kept coming up: a recurring blocker, a recurring win?

Next Week's Focus — what's the one thing that has to happen next week? Set it during the Weekly Planning Ritual.

3

Monthly Log

A once-a-month check-in, four prompts:

  • Core Goals (pick 2) — what are the two outcomes that would make this month count?
  • Growth Goals (pick 2) — what two things do you want to be measurably better at by the end of the month?
  • Ideas & Open Threads — what's worth capturing now so it doesn't get lost: frustrations, ideas, things to revisit?
  • End-of-Month Wins — what actually happened? Be specific: what got done, who helped, what's the evidence?

Built with work in mind, but the shape doesn't care what you're doing — a training block, a semester, a creative project all fit the same four questions.

Core & Growth Goals — carry them straight into next month's Goal Setting & Priorities.

4

Quarterly Review

Same shape, zoomed out — for a quarter, a half, or a full year. Four prompts:

  • Highlights — what went well this period? What are you proud of?
  • What Slipped — which goals or projects didn't get done, and why?
  • What You Learned — new skills, experiments, things you tried for the first time.

Action Items — what will you actually do differently next period? Turn the big ones into OKRs for next quarter.

Retrospectives, Post-Mortems, and Pre-Mortems run the same shape — but they're team/project rituals, not personal cadences, so they live under Processes → Agile.

Capturing Impact

The prompts above tell you what to write about. These two formats are how to actually write it, when the goal is telling the story of what you did — one short, one detailed:

1

SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact)

Originally built for giving feedback, but works just as well for telling your own story — the quick, interview-ready version of an accomplishment. Think of the moment someone asks "tell me about a time you..." and you have thirty seconds to answer well:

  • Situation — set the scene in one sentence: what was going on?
  • Behavior — what did you specifically do? Own it — "I," not "we."
  • Impact — why did it matter, to the outcome, the team, or the business?
2

STARR (Situation-Task-Action-Result-Reflection)

An extension of the classic STAR interview format, with a Reflection step added — useful for documenting your own accomplishments with enough detail to actually be useful later:

  • Situation — what was the context?
  • Task — what were you responsible for?
  • Action — what did you specifically do?
  • Result — what happened because of it? Be specific — numbers, outcomes, who noticed.
  • Reflection — what would you do differently, or what did this teach you?

Try It

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, or whatever AI tool you use:

I want to build a reflection habit. Ask me how often I actually want to check in — daily, weekly, monthly, or quarterly — then help me draft my first entry using that cadence's prompts. If I'm trying to write up something I accomplished, help me frame it using SBI for a quick version or STARR for a detailed one.
