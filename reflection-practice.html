Reflection Practice

The starting point of the cycle — and the theme of this whole site. Know where you are before you decide what to change.

Why It Matters

You can't course-correct what you never looked at. A quick check-in — daily, weekly, monthly, or quarterly, or honestly all of the above at different scales — is what catches a problem early enough to actually fix it, instead of noticing the same mistake months later, after it's already happened a dozen times. Reflection isn't the reward for finishing Plan and Do; it's the step that makes the next round of both worth running.

Tools & Systems

The practice matters more than the tool. Bullet journaling is one on-ramp; Notion, plain paper, or any other system runs the same loop just as well. Two concrete starting points:

1

Bullet Journaling

Rapid logging in brief daily entries, migrated forward instead of left to pile up in old notebooks — a deliberately low-tech system for running Plan-Do-Reflect on paper. Worth its own deep dive later; for now, the short version is: write it down, whether digital or analog — calendar, post-its, or a list — the format matters less than the habit of writing it down at all.

2

Monthly Log

A once-a-month check-in, four prompts:

  • Core Goals (pick 2) — what are the two outcomes that would make this month count?
  • Growth Goals (pick 2) — what two things do you want to be measurably better at by the end of the month?
  • Ideas & Open Threads — what's worth capturing now so it doesn't get lost: frustrations, ideas, things to revisit?
  • End-of-Month Wins — what actually happened? Be specific: what got done, who helped, what's the evidence?

Built with work in mind, but the shape doesn't care what you're doing — a training block, a semester, a creative project all fit the same four questions.
