Your Next Five Moves

Master the Art of Business Strategy

by Patrick Bet-David

2020 · 8h 6m audiobook · ★ 4.7 Audible

The big idea

Business strategy can be taught like a chess game -- thinking several moves ahead about competitors, timing, and scale -- drawing on Bet-David's own path building PHP Agency and Valuetainment rather than academic theory.

The Five Moves Framework
Bet-David structures the book around five progressive moves: knowing yourself and setting a clear, specific goal; building the systems and organizational structure needed to scale past the founder's personal bandwidth; building and leading a high-performing team; developing actual competitive strategy; and mastering the emotional intelligence needed to execute under pressure. Each move is illustrated with business history case studies and Bet-David's personal anecdotes, co-written with Greg Dinkin.

The framework's moves overlap significantly with general leadership and management advice rather than being a distinctly strategic methodology throughout, and its case examples skew heavily toward aggressive, high-growth entrepreneurial contexts.

Key ideas

  1. Aggressive expansion as a default posture

    The book's underlying philosophy favors calculated, ambitious risk-taking and rapid scaling over measured, conservative growth.

  2. Self-awareness before strategy

    Bet-David argues most people can't out-execute a strategy their goals and self-knowledge aren't actually aligned with.

  3. Systems over heroics

    As move two, the book pushes founders to replace their own personal effort with repeatable systems before scaling further.

  4. Reading people as a strategic skill

    Team-building is treated as a competitive advantage in itself, with frameworks for sorting collaborators by motivation and skill type.

  5. Thinking several moves ahead

    The title's core metaphor -- anticipating not just your next move but competitors' and the market's likely responses several steps out.

  6. Timing and emotional discipline as strategic variables

    The final move frames when to act, and how to regulate your own reactions under pressure, as strategic factors as important as the plan itself.

Try it this week

  1. Write a specific, falsifiable one-year goal for your business as the book's first move.
  2. Identify one task only you currently do that should become a documented system or be delegated this month.
  3. Map your current team by motivation/skill type using the book's people-reading framework and flag one obvious mismatch.
  4. Before your next competitive decision, write out what you expect competitors' next two responses to be.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • First-time or early-stage entrepreneurs who want an accessible, story-driven strategy primer rooted in one operator's real experience.
  • Fans of Bet-David's Valuetainment content who want the long-form version of his frameworks.

Less useful if

  • Readers in large, established organizations where the book's founder-centric, aggressive-scaling advice doesn't map onto corporate strategy.
  • Those seeking rigorous, evidence-based or academically vetted strategy frameworks.
