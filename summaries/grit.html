Grit

The Power of Passion and Perseverance

by Angela Duckworth

2016 · 9h 22m audiobook · ★ 4.7 Audible

The big idea

Long-term achievement depends less on innate talent than on grit, the combination of sustained passion and perseverance toward long-term goals, and grit can be deliberately cultivated.

The grit formula
Duckworth models achievement in two steps: Talent times Effort equals Skill, then Skill times Effort equals Achievement, meaning effort counts twice, so it mathematically outweighs raw talent in determining results.

This is Duckworth's own simplified model built to make a psychological point vividly, not a peer-reviewed formula; critics note it's illustrative rather than literally tested.

Key ideas

  1. Grit equals passion plus perseverance

    Sustained, focused commitment to a single long-term goal matters more than raw giftedness.

  2. The four psychological assets

    Gritty people share interest, genuine enjoyment; practice, deliberate daily improvement; purpose, belief the work matters to others; and hope, belief that effort can improve outcomes after setbacks.

  3. Deliberate practice

    Grit is expressed through targeted, effortful practice on weaknesses with immediate feedback, not just putting in hours.

  4. Talent is overrated, relatively

    People consistently overvalue naturals over hard workers even while claiming to believe the opposite.

  5. Grit can be grown

    Through both cultivating interest, practice, purpose, and hope from the inside, and through culture, mentors, and demanding-but-supportive parenting from the outside.

  6. The hard thing rule

    Duckworth's own family rule: everyone must do one hard, deliberately practiced thing and can't quit mid-commitment period, but can choose what that thing is.

Try it this week

  1. Write down your single long-term goal and one hard thing you'll commit to for the next 3 months without quitting.
  2. Schedule 20 minutes of deliberate practice this week on the specific weak point of a skill you're building, with a way to get feedback.
  3. Write one sentence connecting your current work to how it helps someone else.
  4. Track one small daily win related to your goal for 7 days to build the habit of seeing effort produce results.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • People chasing a long-term goal who need motivation to keep going
  • Parents, coaches, and managers interested in developing perseverance in others

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting a general anti-procrastination or daily productivity toolkit rather than long-horizon commitment
  • Skeptics of self-help psychology, since the book's core research has drawn published academic criticism about effect size and predictive power
