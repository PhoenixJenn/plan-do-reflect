HBR Emotional Intelligence Series, Confidence

by Harvard Business Review

2022 · 1h 56m audiobook · ★ 4.3 Audible

The big idea

Confidence is a buildable skill rather than a fixed trait: the collected HBR articles argue that it comes from deliberate practice, mental preparation, and honest self-assessment, and that both too little confidence and too much undermine leadership effectiveness.

The Confidence-Competence Practice Loop
Several articles in the collection converge on the same mechanism: confidence grows from a cycle of attempting a task, getting feedback, and incrementally improving competence, not from positive thinking alone. Applied version: set a small, specific challenge slightly outside your comfort zone, get real feedback on the attempt, and repeat, so confidence tracks actual demonstrated skill rather than outrunning it.

This is not a single-author book but an HBR-curated anthology of previously published articles by different contributors, so treat the synthesis below as common threads across its articles rather than one author's unified claim.

Key ideas

  1. Imposter syndrome is near-universal

    Even highly accomplished professionals routinely doubt their qualifications, and normalizing this doubt, rather than treating it as a personal flaw, is the first step to managing it.

  2. The beginner's bubble is a real risk

    Research cited in the collection found novices sometimes rate their own performance far higher than actual performance, showing early overconfidence can block real learning.

  3. Mental preparation techniques from athletes transfer to work

    Visualization, controlled breathing, positive self-talk, and pre-performance rituals are presented as ways to manage performance anxiety before high-stakes moments.

  4. Confidence and competence aren't the same thing

    One widely cited piece asks why so many incompetent people become leaders, arguing organizations often mistake confident behavior for actual competence.

Try it this week

  1. Before your next presentation, use one athlete-style ritual: a two-minute breathing routine plus a specific visualization of the opening thirty seconds going well.
  2. Identify one task this week where your actual skill likely exceeds your stated confidence, and consciously advocate for yourself as capably as you'd advocate for a colleague.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Managers who want short, article-length reads on a single theme rather than a full book
  • People specifically wrestling with imposter syndrome or preparing for a high-stakes presentation or interview

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting one continuous argument or narrative voice, since this is a multi-author anthology
  • Anyone wanting deep academic rigor on confidence research rather than HBR's magazine-style practitioner treatment
