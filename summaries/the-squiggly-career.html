The Squiggly Career

The No.1 Sunday Times Business Bestseller - Ditch the Ladder, Discover Opportunity, Design Your Career

by Helen Tupper, Sarah Ellis

2020 · 5h 27m audiobook · ★ 4.2 Audible

The big idea

Careers no longer move in a predictable straight line up a ladder; they move in unpredictable squiggles across roles, industries, and skills, and thriving in that environment requires proactive self-knowledge and adaptability rather than waiting for a traditional promotion path.

The Four Career Questions (super strengths, values, motivation, direction)
Tupper and Ellis structure the book around four questions: What am I good at? (identifying super strengths, the intersection of skill and enjoyment), What do I stand for? (clarifying personal values), What motivates and drives me? (distinguishing intrinsic drivers from externally imposed goals), and Where do I want to go? (mapping possible future directions without forcing a single linear path). Each comes with exercises drawn from the authors' work at Amazing If and interviews with professionals who've navigated nonlinear careers.

The four-question framework is built from the authors' coaching practice and podcast interviews rather than formal academic research, and offers less concrete guidance for someone who needs an immediate, one-time career decision.

Key ideas

  1. The ladder metaphor is broken

    The authors argue traditional linear career-ladder thinking creates unnecessary anxiety when real careers already move sideways, backward, and diagonally.

  2. Super strengths sit at skill-meets-energy

    Their concept of a super strength is specifically the overlap between what you're good at and what energizes you.

  3. Confidence is built through small actions, not felt first

    The book treats self-doubt as something addressed through incremental action and evidence-gathering rather than waiting to feel confident before acting.

  4. Values should filter opportunities, not just guide big decisions

    Tupper and Ellis push readers to use clarified personal values as a day-to-day filter for smaller choices.

  5. Networks should be built before you need them

    The book emphasizes proactively building a support network ahead of a transition.

  6. Career direction is a set of possibilities, not one plan

    Rather than a single five-year plan, the authors encourage mapping multiple possible directions simultaneously.

Try it this week

  1. Write down three super strengths this week by listing tasks where you're both skilled and energized, then look for one way to do more of one of them.
  2. Draft a short list of your top three work values and use them to evaluate one upcoming decision this week.
  3. Identify one person outside your immediate team or function to have a genuine squiggly networking conversation with this month.
  4. Instead of writing a single five-year plan, sketch two or three different possible directions your role could take over the next two years.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Professionals feeling anxious about not following a traditional promotion timeline who want language and tools to reframe that as normal.
  • Early- to mid-career employees navigating frequent role or industry changes who want structured self-assessment exercises.

Less useful if

  • Senior executives with highly specialized, linear career paths who need advice on board-level positioning.
  • Readers wanting rigorous data or academic research on career trajectories rather than a coaching-derived framework.
