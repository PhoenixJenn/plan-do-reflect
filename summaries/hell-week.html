Hell Week

Seven Days to Be Your Best Self

by Erik Bertrand Larssen

2016 · 4h 57m audiobook · ★ 4.6 Audible

The big idea

A single, deliberately structured week of extreme discipline, modeled loosely on the grueling Hell Week of Navy SEAL training, can act as a catalyst that resets your habits and proves you're capable of far more than your comfort zone suggests.

The Hell Week challenge
For seven consecutive days, commit to non-negotiable baseline rules: wake at 5:00 a.m. every day including weekends, lights out at 10:00 p.m., only healthy food, no TV or social media during work hours, and at least one hour of exercise daily, layered with your own specific goal for the week.

Larssen is a Norwegian mental-performance coach who has worked with Olympic athletes and executives; the book is more a personal-discipline challenge than an academically grounded framework, so read its claims as coaching rather than research.

Key ideas

  1. Structure without purpose is empty

    The rigid rules only work if paired with a clearly defined personal goal you're using the week to pursue.

  2. Adversity as fuel

    Discomfort and difficulty are reframed as opportunities and growth signals rather than problems to avoid.

  3. Radical consistency beats motivation

    Sticking to the same wake time, diet, and exercise every day, weekends included, removes decision fatigue and builds momentum.

  4. Eliminate escape hatches

    Cutting off TV and social media during the week removes the passive-consumption habits that normally absorb willpower and time.

  5. One week can reframe a life

    Because a typical life is roughly 4,000 weeks long, Larssen argues one intensely disciplined week can permanently shift your self-image and standards.

  6. Build a support system

    Identify people who will hold you accountable and help you sustain the changes after the week ends.

Try it this week

  1. Pick one specific, personal goal for a 7-day challenge before you start, not just be disciplined, something concrete.
  2. Set a fixed wake time for the next 7 days, including the weekend, and don't move it.
  3. Cut TV and social media entirely during your designated work hours for one week and track how much extra time appears.
  4. Commit to one hour of exercise daily for a week and note the effect on your energy and mood at day 7.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • People who respond well to intense, short-burst challenges and military-style discipline
  • Readers looking for a jolt to break out of a rut

Less useful if

  • People who need gradual, sustainable habit-building rather than an all-or-nothing sprint
  • Anyone with health or scheduling constraints that make the rigid rules unrealistic
