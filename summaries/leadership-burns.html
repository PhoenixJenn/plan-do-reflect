Leadership

by James MacGregor Burns

1978 · ★ 3.85 Goodreads (506)

The big idea

Leadership is a relationship, not a trait or a title — it only exists where followers consent. Burns draws the line that later became famous: transactional leadership trades one thing for another, while transformational leadership raises both leader and follower to a higher level of purpose and motivation.

Transactional vs. transformational
Transactional: an exchange — votes for promises, labor for wages. Transformational: leader and follower pull each other toward a shared purpose that outlasts the exchange itself.

Every later "leadership style" framework on this site, including Goleman's Six, builds on this original split.

Key ideas

  1. Leadership requires consent

    Burns distinguishes leadership from naked power: a leader needs followers who choose to follow, not just the capacity to coerce.

  2. Transactional leadership

    An exchange of value where leader and follower pursue separate but related goals — necessary, but limited to the terms of the deal.

  3. Transformational leadership

    Leader and follower engage each other in a way that raises both to a higher level of motivation and, in Burns's own framing, morality.

  4. Leadership and followership are inseparable

    You can't study leaders in isolation — the relationship is the unit of analysis, not the individual at the top.

  5. A political scientist's lens on a business idea

    Written about presidents and movements, not companies — the ideas transferred to management decades later largely intact.

Try it this week

  1. List your current working relationships and mark each one transactional or transformational.
  2. Pick one purely transactional relationship and ask what larger purpose it could ladder up to.
  3. Next time you delegate, pair the transactional ask (the "what") with a transformational frame (the "why").

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Readers who want the original theoretical grounding behind "transformational leadership," before it became a business buzzword
  • Anyone who wants to understand leadership as a relationship, not a position

Less useful if

  • Looking for a quick, practical read — this is dense political science, not a business how-to
  • Primarily interested in team-level tactics rather than the underlying theory
