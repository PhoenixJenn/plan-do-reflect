Pitch Anything

An Innovative Method for Presenting, Persuading, and Winning the Deal

by Oren Klaff

2022 · 6h 14m audiobook · ★ 4.8 Audible

The big idea

Pitches are won or lost in the listener's primitive, emotional brain rather than through rational analysis, so Klaff argues you win by controlling the social frame of the interaction and making the other side chase you, not the reverse.

The STRONG method
A six-step pitch structure: Set the frame, Tell the story, Reveal the intrigue, Offer the prize, Nail the hook point, Get a decision, designed to trigger a fast emotional response before the listener's analytical brain can pick the pitch apart.

Illustrated throughout with Klaff's own high-stakes deal pitches as an investment banker; multiple reviewers flag the underlying frame-control and neediness-eradication tactics as bordering on manipulative.

Key ideas

  1. Three-brain model

    Pitches must satisfy the primal, emotional brain first: simplicity and novelty beat detail and data.

  2. Frame control

    Whoever holds the stronger social frame in a room controls the interaction; weaker frames collapse into stronger ones.

  3. Become the prize

    Flip the dynamic so the buyer or investor is qualifying themselves to you, not the other way around.

  4. Hot cognitions

    People make fast emotional decisions and rationalize them afterward, so intrigue and mild time pressure drive action.

  5. Eradicate neediness

    Visible desperation signals low status and kills deals; detachment reads as power.

  6. Status moves

    Subtle power plays, like reclaiming attention and not over-explaining, run throughout a meeting, not just the opening.

Try it this week

  1. Before your next pitch or ask, write one sentence simple enough for a 10-year-old to grasp.
  2. In your next negotiation, find one thing you're over-explaining and cut it.
  3. Notice this week when you're over-explaining in conversation and consciously stop.
  4. Before an important meeting, decide what you're genuinely willing to walk away from.
