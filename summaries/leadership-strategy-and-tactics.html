Leadership Strategy and Tactics

Field Manual

by Jocko Willink

2020 · 8h 25m audiobook · ★ 4.9 Audible

The big idea

Unlike Extreme Ownership's foundational principles, this is a practical, scenario-based field manual, short, situation-specific chapters give leaders at any level immediately actionable tactics for real day-to-day problems: managing up, down, and sideways, and staying level-headed under pressure.

Detach (within Prioritize and Execute)
When a leader is hit with multiple problems at once, Willink prescribes literally and mentally stepping back from the tactical noise to gain a wider view, rank the problems by priority, and tackle only the single highest-priority issue before returning to the rest. In a team setting this might mean physically stepping out of the immediate action; in an office setting it might mean pausing before reacting to a flood of demands. The habit keeps a leader from getting pulled into one problem's vortex while a bigger one goes unaddressed.

Prioritize and Execute is one of Willink's Four Laws of Combat, introduced conceptually in Extreme Ownership, this Field Manual's distinct contribution is walking through concrete, non-combat workplace scenarios for applying it.

Key ideas

  1. Scenario-based structure

    Short chapters tackle specific situational problems, a micromanaging boss, an underperforming peer, imposter syndrome, rather than laying out abstract theory.

  2. Leading down

    Build people up by identifying their individual strengths, pushing them outside their comfort zone deliberately, and letting them build their own plans rather than dictating solutions.

  3. Leading sideways

    Manage peer relationships by picking battles carefully and building trust and influence without relying on formal authority or ego.

  4. Leading up

    Specific tactics for handling different types of difficult bosses without damaging the relationship.

  5. Default: Aggressive mindset

    A bias toward proactively engaging problems, balanced against the Dichotomy of Leadership's need for restraint.

  6. Managing praise, criticism, and hope tactically

    Deliberately calibrating how and when to give feedback or encouragement to a specific person and situation, not a one-size-fits-all approach.

Try it this week

  1. Next time you're hit with simultaneous problems, physically step back for two minutes, rank them, and attack only the top one first.
  2. Identify a peer relationship where you're avoiding a small conflict, and pick that one battle to address directly this week.
  3. If you have a difficult boss, identify which type fits best and apply one specific tactic for that type.
  4. Give one team member a stretch assignment this week and let them build their own plan for it instead of handing them the steps.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Frontline and mid-level managers who want concrete, situation-by-situation tactics rather than philosophy
  • Readers who've already read Extreme Ownership and want the how layered onto the why

Less useful if

  • Readers new to Willink who haven't read Extreme Ownership first, since this book assumes familiarity with the Four Laws of Combat
  • Readers wanting research citations rather than military-anecdote-driven advice
