Million Dollar Habits

Proven Power Practices to Double and Triple Your Income

by Brian Tracy

2020 · 8h 3m audiobook · ★ 4.7 Audible

The big idea

Success in wealth, career, health, and relationships isn't random, it's the product of specific, learnable habits, and the foundation of all of them is a strong self-concept: how you see your ideal self, your current self, and your self-esteem.

The self-concept triangle
Self-ideal, the person you aspire to be, shapes self-image, how you currently see yourself, which in turn shapes self-esteem, how much you value yourself as your self-image moves toward your self-ideal.

Tracy treats this as the psychological engine behind every other habit in the book, presented as assertion more than tested theory.

Key ideas

  1. Habits are learned, not fixed

    Habits are changeable patterns driven by expected consequences and reinforced by emotional investment, so replacing one means changing both the trigger and the payoff.

  2. Most behavior is habitual

    Tracy asserts a very high share of thoughts and behaviors are habitual, a figure later reviewers flag as overstated compared to behavioral research, but it's a useful provocation for taking habits seriously.

  3. The 10% savings rule

    Automatically save 10% of all income, or direct half of any raise to savings, as a non-negotiable habit for building financial independence.

  4. The 80/20 rule applied to effort

    Roughly 80% of your results come from 20% of your activities, so the habit is ruthlessly identifying and prioritizing that 20%.

  5. Modeling successful people

    Rather than trial and error, study and copy the specific habits of people who've already achieved what you want.

  6. Relationship habits

    Tracy claims a large share of happiness comes from relationships, built through habits like genuine praise, active listening, and making people feel valued.

Try it this week

  1. Set up an automatic transfer so 10% of your next paycheck goes straight to savings.
  2. Write your top three goals by hand every morning this week.
  3. Do a quick 80/20 audit of your task list and protect time for the 20% producing most results.
  4. Pick one person and practice one specific relationship habit, like full listening without interrupting, daily this week.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Readers wanting broad, practical self-improvement across money, career, health, and relationships in one book
  • People who like simple rules and daily disciplines over deep theory

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting evidence-based behavioral psychology; the book leans motivational and anecdotal, and some claims don't hold up well against current research
  • Some financial advice feels dated and can oversimplify complex situations
