The High 5 Habit

Take Control of Your Life with One Simple Habit

by Mel Robbins

2021 · 6h 57m audiobook · ★ 4.9 Audible

The big idea

A single, simple daily ritual, high-fiving yourself in the mirror, can retrain your brain out of habitual self-criticism and into self-support, and that shift in how you treat yourself is what unlocks the confidence to pursue your goals.

The mirror high five
Every morning, look yourself in the eye and give your reflection a high five, instead of scanning for flaws.

Robbins ties this to pairing an ordinary routine moment with an unexpected, positive gesture; it's her own framing, blending personal narrative with cited research rather than original studies.

Key ideas

  1. Self-positivity bias

    Robbins cites research that people who hold generally favorable views of themselves report lower stress and higher motivation; the high five nudges you toward that bias.

  2. Cue, routine, reward

    The ritual is built on the classic habit-loop model: the mirror is the cue, the high five is the routine, and the resulting feeling is the reward.

  3. What you notice shapes what you see

    Chronic negative self-talk is framed as narrowing your attention toward threats and flaws, while positive self-talk redirects it toward opportunities.

  4. Self-talk shapes action

    Robbins shares her own story of turning down opportunities out of fear, arguing that what you tell yourself before you act directly shapes whether you act at all.

  5. Reframing language

    Swapping deficit language, like I can't, for possibility language, like I'll figure it out, as a deliberate, repeatable practice.

  6. A stacked morning routine

    The high five is paired with other small disciplines, like not hitting snooze or getting morning light, to reinforce a sense of control over the day.

Try it this week

  1. Each morning this week, before checking your phone, high-five yourself in the mirror and say one encouraging phrase out loud.
  2. Write a short list of your personal strengths and keep it somewhere visible.
  3. Catch yourself using 'I can't' language once a day and consciously reframe it to a possibility statement.
  4. Pair the high five with one other small morning discipline to build the routine as a stack.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Readers dealing with low self-confidence or a harsh inner critic who want a low-effort entry point
  • People who respond well to simple, ritual-based habits rather than complex systems

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting rigorous, clinically tested behavioral science; the mechanism is more popular-science shorthand than established clinical fact
  • Anyone already comfortable with self-compassion practices or looking for a full productivity system
