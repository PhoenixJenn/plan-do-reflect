The First-Time Manager

by Jim McCormick

2020 · 7h 40m audiobook · ★ 4.5 Audible

The big idea

New managers succeed not by mastering complex theory but by getting the fundamentals right early -- building trust with former peers, learning to delegate, running fair performance conversations, and recognizing that they'll now be judged by their team's output, not their own.

Situational Management with A/B/C task prioritization
The book teaches new managers to vary their approach based on the specific situation and person in front of them. It offers a simple A/B/C task-prioritization system: A-tasks urgent and important, B-tasks matter but aren't time-critical, C-tasks delegated or deferred. It pairs this with concrete early-tenure moves: one-on-ones with every team member in the first two months, an explicit attitude talk for new hires, and a closed period of protected, interruption-free time.

The book is intentionally broad and introductory (43 short chapters), so tools like the A/B/C system are simple by design and won't satisfy managers who already have prioritization habits.

Key ideas

  1. You're judged by your team's output now

    The book's opening reframe is that a new manager's success is measured by what the team produces, not by their own individual contribution.

  2. Managing former peers

    A dedicated section addresses the awkwardness of managing people who were recently equals, with advice on resetting the relationship.

  3. The attitude talk

    New hires get an explicit conversation about how attitude, not just skill, separates strong performers from mediocre ones.

  4. Delegating differently for different people

    The book stresses that delegation isn't one-size-fits-all -- it should flex based on a given employee's competence and confidence on that specific task.

  5. Surprise-free performance reviews

    Formal reviews are framed as a summary of things the employee should have already heard, never a venue for new criticism.

  6. The closed period

    A scheduled, protected block of uninterrupted time is presented as a concrete tactic for new managers whose calendars get consumed by others' urgent requests.

Try it this week

  1. Sort your current task list into A/B/C priority tiers this week and delegate or defer everything in the C tier.
  2. Schedule individual one-on-ones with every team member you haven't yet had a real conversation with, aiming to complete them within two months.
  3. Block one recurring closed period on your calendar this week and protect it from meetings and interruptions.
  4. Before your next performance review conversation, check that every point you plan to raise has already been said to that person in an earlier one-on-one.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Someone stepping into their very first people-management role, especially one managing former peers.
  • Managers who want a reference they can dip into chapter by chapter for a specific near-term problem.

Less useful if

  • Experienced managers looking for advanced or novel frameworks.
  • Readers wanting research-backed or academically rigorous management theory.
