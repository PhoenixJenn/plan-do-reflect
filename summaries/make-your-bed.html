Make Your Bed

Little Things That Can Change Your Life...And Maybe the World

by William H. McRaven

2017 · 1h 53m audiobook · ★ 4.7 Audible

The big idea

Admiral William McRaven, drawing on Navy SEAL training, argues that changing the world, or your own life, starts with small, disciplined daily actions and requires leaning on others: life is a small rubber boat that can't be paddled alone.

The ten lessons from SEAL training
The book is structured as ten short, image-driven lessons drawn directly from Basic Underwater Demolition and SEAL training, each ending with a variation of if you want to change the world: make your bed, find someone to help you paddle, don't ever, ever ring the bell.

Originally a 2014 University of Texas commencement speech that went viral before being expanded into the book.

Key ideas

  1. Make your bed

    Completing one small task first thing every morning builds momentum and gives you an accomplishment to return to even if the rest of the day goes badly.

  2. Find someone to help you paddle

    SEAL training is done in seven-man boat crews; no meaningful goal is achieved solo, so build and lean on a team.

  3. Only the size of your heart matters

    Physical size or credentials matter less than determination; the smallest, least impressive trainees often outlast bigger, stronger ones.

  4. Get over being a sugar cookie

    Sometimes life is unfair and you'll be set back for reasons outside your control; you have to keep going anyway.

  5. Don't back down from the sharks

    Facing real danger or intimidating obstacles requires confronting fear head-on with preparation and calm rather than panic.

  6. Never, ever ring the bell

    In SEAL training, ringing a bell means quitting; giving up is the only true failure, and persistence through hardship is what separates people who succeed.

Try it this week

  1. Make your bed, or complete one small fixed task, first thing tomorrow morning, every day this week.
  2. Identify one goal you've been trying to do solo and ask one specific person for help with it this week.
  3. When something unfair happens this week, consciously choose to keep moving instead of dwelling on the injustice.
  4. Write down one moment from your past week where you were tempted to quit, and note what you did instead.
