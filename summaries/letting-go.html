Letting Go

The Pathway of Surrender

by David R. Hawkins

2015 · 12h 23m audiobook · ★ 4.6 Audible

The big idea

Suppressed and unexpressed emotions are the hidden engine behind stress and stuck patterns. Hawkins argues that consciously surrendering to a feeling, letting it be fully felt without suppressing, expressing, or escaping it, dissolves its hold, typically within minutes.

The map of consciousness
Hawkins ranks emotional states on a calibrated scale from 1 to 1000, from Shame and Guilt up through Courage at 200, then Neutrality, Acceptance, Love, and Peace; 200 is the pivot point where a person shifts from states that drain energy to states that generate it.

The scale was derived using applied-kinesiology muscle testing, a method that hasn't held up in blinded scientific trials, so treat the numeric calibrations as a symbolic, motivational device rather than a validated measurement.

Key ideas

  1. Three ways people mishandle feelings

    Most people cope with hard emotions through distraction, burying them until they resurface as anxiety or illness, or venting onto others, none of which actually resolves the feeling.

  2. Surrender vs. suppression vs. expression

    The release technique means neither pushing a feeling down nor acting it out, but consciously allowing it to be present until its energy runs its course.

  3. Resistance feeds the emotion

    Fighting the emotion is what gives it power and allows it to grow; the more you resist a feeling, the longer it persists.

  4. One feeling, many thoughts

    A single buried emotion can generate an endless stream of related anxious thoughts; addressing the root feeling is more effective than chasing each thought.

  5. I can't often means I won't

    Many perceived limitations are disguised unwillingness; recognizing this restores a sense of choice and agency.

  6. Wanting pushes away

    Intense craving or desperate desire for an outcome creates distance from it; releasing the neediness behind a goal paradoxically makes it easier to reach.

Try it this week

  1. Pick one recurring negative feeling this week and, instead of distracting or venting, sit with it for a few minutes and let yourself feel it fully.
  2. Next time you say I can't, pause and rephrase it as I won't or I'm choosing not to, and notice if that changes how you feel.
  3. When you notice yourself gripping tightly to a desired outcome, practice consciously relaxing your need for that specific result.
  4. Keep a one-line log for a week noting which avoidance pattern, distract, bury, or vent, you default to under stress.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Readers drawn to introspective, spiritual self-help
  • People dealing with chronic stress or anxiety patterns

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting evidence-based psychology or clinical rigor
  • Anyone skeptical of muscle-testing or energy-frequency claims
