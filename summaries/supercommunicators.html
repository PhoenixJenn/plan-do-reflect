Supercommunicators

How to Unlock the Secret Language of Connection

by Charles Duhigg

2024 · 7h 28m audiobook · ★ 4.4 Audible

The big idea

Every meaningful conversation is actually one of three types, practical, emotional, or social, happening at once, and supercommunicators succeed by figuring out which type is occurring and deliberately matching that mode rather than responding on a different wavelength.

The matching principle, with looping for understanding
Identify whether a conversation is practical, what's this really about; emotional, how do we feel; or social, who are we; then match the other person's mode. Duhigg pairs this with looping for understanding: ask a question, repeat back what you heard in your own words, and confirm you got it right.

Illustrated with research and real cases ranging from hostage negotiators to jury deliberations, drawing on the neuroscience of neural entrainment between well-matched conversation partners.

Key ideas

  1. Three conversation types

    Practical, emotional, and social conversations each require a different mode of listening and response.

  2. Looping for understanding

    Repeat back what you heard before responding, to demonstrate genuine listening rather than assumed understanding.

  3. Perspective-getting over perspective-taking

    Directly asking someone how they feel beats guessing or imagining their perspective.

  4. Neural entrainment

    In well-matched conversations, people's brain activity, breathing, and even pupils sync up, a measurable marker of connection.

  5. Deep questions

    Asking about values and experiences instead of surface facts unlocks more honest, connective exchanges.

  6. Multiple identities

    Reminding people of their other identities, parent, teammate, neighbor, can defuse identity-based conflict.

Try it this week

  1. In your next disagreement, pause and ask yourself whether it's a practical, emotional, or identity conversation before responding.
  2. Practice looping: after someone explains something important, repeat it back in your own words and check if you got it right.
  3. Replace one assumption this week with a direct deep question instead of guessing how someone feels.
  4. In a tense conversation, name a shared identity or goal out loud to reset the footing.
