The First 90 Days

Proven Strategies for Getting Up to Speed Faster and Smarter

by Michael D. Watkins

2013 · 6h 58m audiobook · ★ 4.3 Audible

The big idea

Michael Watkins argues that the actions a leader takes in the first few months of a new role disproportionately determine long-term success or failure, and that deliberately managing this transition period, rather than relying on what worked in a past role, can significantly accelerate the time to full productivity.

The STARS Model
STARS categorizes the business situation a new leader is stepping into as Start-up, Turnaround, Accelerated growth, Realignment, or Sustaining success, each demanding a different strategy, pace, and set of priorities. Watkins pairs STARS with the 5 Ps (products, processes, projects, plants and facilities, and people) so leaders can diagnose which situation they're actually in across each dimension, since a single unit can be a turnaround in one area and sustaining success in another.

Watkins later expanded STARS in subsequent editions to note that leaders often face a portfolio of several STARS situations simultaneously across different parts of their remit, rather than one clean category for the whole role.

Key ideas

  1. Break-even point

    New leaders typically take months to reach the point where they've contributed as much value as they've consumed in support and resources; deliberate transition management shortens this window.

  2. The 30-30-30 structure

    Watkins recommends breaking the 90 days into roughly month-long phases, learning and building credibility, generating early wins, and then deepening and consolidating change.

  3. Negotiating success with your boss

    Early, explicit conversations about situational diagnosis, expectations, resources, and working style prevent misalignment that surfaces later as conflict.

  4. Securing early wins

    Identifying a few visible, meaningful improvements in the first weeks builds credibility and momentum for tackling harder, longer-term challenges.

  5. Matching strategy to situation

    A strategy that worked in a turnaround can actively damage a sustaining-success situation, leaders must diagnose before acting.

  6. Building your team early

    New leaders should assess existing team members quickly, decide who stays, who moves, and who needs support, rather than deferring people decisions indefinitely.

Try it this week

  1. Before or right after starting a new role, write down which STARS situation you believe you're stepping into for each major area of responsibility.
  2. Schedule an explicit expectations conversation with your new manager in your first two weeks, covering how they define success in 90 days.
  3. Identify one visible, achievable win you could deliver in your first 30 days and start on it immediately.
  4. List your new team members and rate, even roughly, who you believe is a keep, develop, or move decision, revisiting the list after 60 days.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Anyone starting a new leadership role, promotion, or job in the coming months
  • HR and onboarding teams designing transition-support programs

Less useful if

  • Individual contributors without team or organizational responsibility, since the STARS/5Ps framework is built around managing people and units
  • Readers wanting a light read, as the book is dense with checklists and frameworks
