What's Next Is Now

How to Live Future Ready

by Frederik Pferdt

2024 · 7h 0m audiobook · ★ 4.8 Audible

The big idea

The future isn't something that happens to us or that we predict, it's something we actively create through a future-ready mindstate built on openness, curiosity, experimentation, empathy, and radical optimism, applied through small, intentional daily choices.

Collecting and connecting dots
Pferdt frames creativity as a practice of deliberately collecting dots, new experiences, observations, and perspectives gathered through small, intentional choices like taking a different route or talking to someone outside your usual circle, and then actively connecting those dots into novel ideas. The tool reframes curiosity and openness as trainable daily habits rather than fixed personality traits.

This is Pferdt's newly published book, and independent, non-promotional secondary coverage is limited compared to more established titles; most summaries found trace back to the author's own site and Blinkist rather than independent critical reviews.

Key ideas

  1. Radical optimism

    Shifting from a passive things-will-work-out mindset to an active I-can-create-possibility-now stance, treated as a deliberate choice rather than an innate disposition.

  2. Unreserved openness

    Favoring a beginner's mind over expert certainty, since repeatedly stepping outside comfort zones builds adaptive capacity over time.

  3. Compulsive curiosity

    Reclaiming persistent questioning, the kind common in childhood, as a strategic professional advantage rather than something to outgrow.

  4. Perpetual experimentation

    Reframing setbacks as data to learn from rather than failures to avoid, encouraging frequent small experiments over rare big bets.

Try it this week

  1. Take a different physical route once this week purely to collect a new dot of observation or experience.
  2. Pick one recurring frustration or setback from the past month and rewrite it in one sentence as data rather than failure.
  3. Identify the smallest possible next step on a stalled project and take it within 48 hours, rather than waiting for ideal conditions.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Individuals and teams wanting a personal-development framework for navigating uncertainty and change
  • Readers interested in innovation culture from a former Google Chief Innovation Evangelist's perspective

Less useful if

  • Readers seeking a rigorously evidence-based or academically sourced text rather than a practice-and-mindset book
  • Those wanting organizational-level frameworks rather than an individual mindset and habit approach
