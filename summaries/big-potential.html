Big Potential

How Transforming the Pursuit of Success Raises Our Achievement, Happiness, and Well-Being

by Shawn Achor

2018 · 6h 35m audiobook · ★ 4.7 Audible

The big idea

Small potential, success pursued in isolation, is capped. Big potential is unlocked by deliberately building the people and ecosystem around you, since achievement, happiness, and well-being rise together only through connection, not competition.

The SEEDS framework
Five practices for growing big potential: Surround yourself with positive, complementary people; Expand your power by giving it away; Enhance others through genuine praise; Defend your team against negativity; and Sustain gains through a virtuous cycle of reinforcement.

Illustrated with case studies like Kaiser Permanente's program empowering front-line receptionists to flag cancer-screening gaps, which reportedly helped save hundreds of lives.

Key ideas

  1. Small potential has a ceiling

    Chasing individual achievement alone caps how high you can rise, since you're limited to your own resources and energy.

  2. Assists beat scoring

    Research Achor cites found basketball assists predict team wins more reliably than individual point totals: collaboration outperforms solo effort.

  3. Leadership from any seat

    You don't need a title to expand influence; empowering people below or beside you multiplies impact more than hoarding authority.

  4. Praise redirected builds trust

    Crediting the team, and even competitors, rather than self-promoting, is shown in one case to be what actually won a partnership.

  5. Cognitive diversity wins

    Teams that combine different thinking styles consistently outperform more homogeneous expert teams.

  6. Motivation fades without reinforcement

    A single burst of inspiration dissipates fast unless you build a support system that sustains it.

Try it this week

  1. Identify one energy-draining relationship or habit this week and consciously limit exposure to it.
  2. Give one piece of specific, public credit to a teammate or collaborator instead of claiming an individual win.
  3. Hand off one small piece of ownership or decision-making to someone else on your team this week.
  4. After learning something useful, teach or share it with one other person within 48 hours to lock in the gain.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Managers and team leads
  • People whose growth feels capped by going it alone

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting rigorous, citation-heavy research; it leans on illustrative anecdotes
  • Readers looking for individual-only habit or productivity advice rather than a team lens
