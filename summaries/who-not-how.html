Who Not How

The Formula to Achieve Bigger Goals Through Accelerating Teamwork

by Dan Sullivan, Benjamin Hardy

2020 · 5h 24m audiobook · ★ 4.8 Audible

The big idea

Most people limit their success by asking how do I do this myself, a question that caps you at your own time, skills, and energy. Asking who can help me do this instead unlocks exponentially bigger goals by leveraging other people's strengths.

The who not how question shift
Whenever you face a goal or problem, replace how will I get this done with who can get this done, turning you from a sole doer into an orchestrator who finds the right collaborator, partner, or expert for each piece of the goal.

Sullivan, via his Strategic Coach program, frames this alongside four freedoms the approach unlocks: time, money, relationship, and purpose, while Hardy supplies the psychological research.

Key ideas

  1. The how trap

    Defaulting to how do I do this keeps you doing everything yourself, diluting focus and capping growth at your personal bandwidth.

  2. Delegate everything except genius

    Focus only on the small set of tasks you're uniquely excellent at and love doing; hand off everything else to the right who.

  3. Four freedoms

    The payoff of consistently finding the right people is greater freedom of time, money, relationships, and purpose.

  4. Collaboration is mutual, not exploitative

    Effective who partnerships work because both sides get real value; it's a win-win exchange, not offloading grunt work onto others.

  5. Vision attracts collaborators

    Clearly articulating what you want to achieve is what draws the right whos to want to help you achieve it.

  6. Self-multiplication through teams

    Like a sports team, combining complementary strengths produces results no individual could achieve alone.

Try it this week

  1. Take one task you dread or are bad at, and write down who, a person, service, or tool, could own it instead.
  2. For one goal you're stuck on, replace how do I with who could help me, and list three possible people.
  3. Identify your own genius zone, the one or two things you do better than almost anything else, and audit how much of your week is spent there.
  4. Write one clear, specific sentence describing a goal you want help with, ready to share with a potential collaborator.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Entrepreneurs and business owners scaling past what they can do solo
  • Overwhelmed professionals doing too much low-leverage work themselves

Less useful if

  • Readers without any budget, team, or network to delegate to
  • Those wanting rigorous academic research rather than an entrepreneurship-coaching framework
