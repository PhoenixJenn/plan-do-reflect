Better and Faster

The Proven Path to Unstoppable Ideas

by Jeremy Gutsche

2015 · 6h 43m audiobook · ★ 4.4 Audible

The big idea

Businesses fail not from lack of talent but from falling into complacent farmer habits; sustained success requires adopting a hunter mindset and a repeatable method for spotting the trends already emerging around you.

The six patterns of opportunity
Drawing on Trend Hunter's analysis of roughly 250,000 ideas, Gutsche distills trend-spotting into six repeatable patterns: Convergence (combining separate trends), Divergence (splitting a category into a specialized niche), Cyclicality (reviving and updating something from the past), Redirection (applying an existing capability to a new market), Reduction (stripping something down to its essentials), and Acceleration (speeding up an existing process). He advises picking a specific hunting ground, a market or category to focus on, and systematically running each pattern against it to surface opportunities.

Exact naming of the six patterns varies slightly across secondary summaries of the book; confirm precise wording against the book itself before quoting verbatim.

Key ideas

  1. Farmer vs. hunter mindset

    Farmer organizations protect what they have and repeat past tactics; hunter organizations stay insatiably curious and are willing to disrupt their own success, and only the latter survives long term.

  2. Three farmer traps

    Complacency, resting on past wins; repetition, assuming old tactics still work; and overprotectiveness, guarding an existing market instead of cannibalizing it.

  3. Three hunter instincts

    Insatiability, always scanning for the next opportunity; curiosity, questioning assumptions; and a willingness to destroy your own current advantage before a competitor does.

  4. Innovation archetypes

    The book includes a self-assessment to identify your own or your team's innovation personality, surfacing blind spots and suggesting tactics suited to that style.

  5. Pattern recognition over prediction

    Rather than trying to predict the future, opportunity comes from recognizing patterns already playing out in adjacent industries and applying them to your own.

  6. Case-driven proof

    The book closes with dozens of short innovation secrets drawn from well-known companies, showing the patterns in action.

Try it this week

  1. Pick one of the six patterns and spend 20 minutes listing products in your industry that could combine with something from a totally different industry.
  2. Write down one thing your team does because that's how we've always done it, and identify which farmer trap it falls into.
  3. Take 15 minutes to define your specific hunting ground for this quarter, instead of trying to watch every trend at once.
  4. Find one recent innovation from an unrelated industry and write a one-paragraph memo on how the same idea could apply to your own work.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Entrepreneurs and innovation teams looking for a structured trend-spotting exercise
  • Leaders auditing why their organization has stalled

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting deep academic rigor behind the research claims, since the book is written for a practitioner audience
  • Those who prefer narrative case studies over frameworks and self-assessments
