Toastmasters Secret

A Practical Guide to Become a Competent Communicator in Public Speaking

by Ramakrishna Reddy

2016 · 2h 16m audiobook · ★ 3.9 Audible

The big idea

New Toastmasters members progress faster through the Competent Communicator manual when they can study model speeches and side-by-side analysis for each of the program's core speech projects, rather than guessing how to meet each project's objectives.

Speech-project analysis table
For each of the ten Competent Communicator projects, the book pairs a full model speech with a table showing exactly how it satisfies that project's stated objectives.

Independently published title with no coverage found from major review outlets, Four Minute Books, Shortform, or Blinkist. The only substantive third-party assessment is mixed Goodreads reviews, and the author's stated credentials could not be independently confirmed beyond his own site.

Key ideas

  1. Model speeches per project

    Each Competent Communicator project gets an example speech to study rather than an abstract description.

  2. Delivery mechanics broken out separately

    Body language, vocal variety, and pacing are treated project by project rather than as one generic lesson.

  3. Topic selection guidance

    The book gives a process for picking speech topics, flagged by reviewers as a genuinely useful section.

  4. Eliminating distracting habits

    Filler words and nervous mannerisms are addressed as their own topic.

Try it this week

  1. Before your next club speech, write one sentence stating that project's core objective and check your draft against it.
  2. Record yourself delivering a 2-minute practice speech and count filler words to establish a baseline.
  3. Draft three possible topics for your next talk and pick the one you can make most personal and concrete.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Brand-new Toastmasters members working through the Competent Communicator manual

Less useful if

  • Anyone outside the Toastmasters program, since the book is built entirely around that specific curriculum
  • Speakers wanting original theory or research rather than a study companion
