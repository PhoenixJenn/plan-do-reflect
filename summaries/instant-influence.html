Instant Influence

How to Get Anyone to Do Anything--Fast

by Michael Pantalon

2011 · 6h 57m audiobook · ★ 4.5 Audible

The big idea

People are far more persuadable when they discover and voice their own reasons for changing than when they're told to change; a short structured series of questions grounded in motivational interviewing can unlock existing, if buried, motivation in minutes.

The 6-Question Instant Influence Method
Ask, in order: on a scale of 1-10, how ready are you to do X; why didn't you pick a lower number; imagine you did decide to do it, what would be the positive outcomes; why are those outcomes important to you personally; what's the next step, if any. The pivot question, why not a lower number, is the core mechanism: it forces the person to generate their own reasons for change out loud, which is more persuasive than being handed reasons by someone else.

Pantalon adapts this from established Motivational Interviewing research developed by Miller and Rollnick; he condensed it into this six-question version rather than originating MI itself.

Key ideas

  1. Psychological Reactance

    Telling someone what to do, even about something they'd otherwise enjoy, triggers instinctive resistance; preserving their sense of choice is what makes the questions work.

  2. Everyone Has Some Readiness

    Even a 1 or 2 on a readiness scale is never a true zero; the method finds and amplifies that small existing spark rather than manufacturing motivation from nothing.

  3. The Paradoxical Question

    Asking why didn't you choose a lower number is more effective than why should you change, because it has the person defend the change rather than defend the status quo.

  4. Speed Matters

    The technique is designed to work in about seven minutes, making it usable in real-world moments rather than requiring a lengthy session.

  5. From Talk to Commitment

    The later questions push past insight toward a concrete next step, turning verbalized motivation into an actual, time-bound action.

  6. Works Across Contexts

    Developed in clinical and addiction-treatment settings, Pantalon extends the approach to parenting, sales, and workplace situations.

Try it this week

  1. Next time you need someone, or yourself, to act, ask the readiness question first, 1 to 10, how ready are you, before making any argument for why they should do it.
  2. When you get a number, follow immediately with why not a lower number and just listen, resisting the urge to add your own reasons.
  3. Use the method on yourself for one task you've been avoiding this week, writing your own answers down.
  4. End any change conversation you have this week with what's the next step, if any, instead of assigning the next step yourself.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Managers, coaches, and parents needing a fast technique for one-on-one conversations
  • People who find they over-argue to persuade others and get resistance in return

Less useful if

  • Readers looking for organizational or large-scale change strategy rather than individual conversations
  • Can feel scripted or manipulative if used without genuine curiosity about the other person's answer
