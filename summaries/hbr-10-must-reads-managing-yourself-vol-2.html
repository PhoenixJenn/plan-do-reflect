HBR's 10 Must Reads on Managing Yourself, Vol. 2

by Harvard Business Review

2021 · 4h 57m audiobook · ★ 3.5 Audible

The big idea

Sustainable career effectiveness comes less from grand self-reinvention than from clarifying your purpose, managing your energy and emotions deliberately, and making values-aligned choices under pressure. Across this anthology's eleven HBR articles, the throughline is: know yourself well enough to direct your own growth, decisions, and boundaries.

Five questions for a tough decision
What are the full consequences of each option? What are my core obligations? What will actually work in the world as it is? Who are we? What can I live with?

From Joseph Badaracco's article 'How to Tackle Your Toughest Decisions.' He frames hard calls as right-versus-right dilemmas that need judgment, not just analysis.

Key ideas

  1. Purpose needs a usable statement

    'From Purpose to Impact' argues an abstract sense of purpose only helps once distilled into a short, concrete statement you can use to evaluate daily decisions and role choices.

  2. Learning is a trainable skill

    'Learning to Learn' reframes being a good learner as curiosity, vulnerability, and seeking feedback, a skillset that can atrophy or be deliberately strengthened as roles change faster than ever.

  3. Play to strengths, not just weaknesses

    Research in 'Making Yourself Indispensable' finds people improve their standing more by amplifying a few genuine strengths than by fixing weaknesses.

  4. Protect time for what matters, not just what's urgent

    'Make Time for the Work That Matters' shows how calendars fill with low-value busywork and offers ways to audit and reclaim time for meaningful work.

  5. Collaboration overload drives burnout

    Research in 'Collaboration Without Burnout' finds excessive meetings, cc's, and requests are a major hidden cause of exhaustion, with ways to redistribute the load.

  6. Unhook from unhelpful emotions

    'Emotional Agility' teaches noticing, labeling, and accepting a thought or feeling as transient data, then acting on your values rather than obeying or fighting it.

  7. Courage is buildable in small increments

    'Cultivating Everyday Courage' reframes workplace courage as small, practicable acts like speaking up or pushing back respectfully, which compound with practice.

Try it this week

  1. Write a one-sentence impact statement of your purpose and test one upcoming decision against it.
  2. Audit your calendar for a week, flag the busywork versus what actually matters, and cancel or delegate one item.
  3. Next time a thought hooks you emotionally, practice labeling it before reacting.
  4. Before your next hard call at work, run it through the five questions instead of defaulting to gut instinct alone.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Mid-career professionals navigating change
  • Readers who like article-length, anthology-style reads over one long book
  • Managers wanting a range of HBR-validated frameworks on self-management

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting one unified system; this is a curated set of standalone articles, not a single cohesive methodology
  • Anyone put off by fairly corporate language and analogies in a few of the essays
