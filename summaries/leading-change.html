Leading Change

An Action Plan from The World's Foremost Expert on Business Leadership

by John Kotter

2011 · 6h 34m audiobook · ★ 4.4 Audible

The big idea

Large-scale organizational change fails or succeeds based on following a specific sequence of eight steps, and skipping steps or moving too fast is the most common reason transformations stall.

The 8-Step Process for Leading Change
Kotter's eight steps run in sequence: create a sense of urgency; build a guiding coalition; form a strategic vision; enlist a volunteer army; enable action by removing barriers; generate short-term wins; sustain acceleration; and institute change by embedding it in culture. Each step builds on the last, a guiding coalition can't recruit a volunteer army until urgency and vision are already established, and Kotter's research found that skipping steps or declaring victory too early is the most common cause of failed change efforts.

Key ideas

  1. Sense of urgency

    Change fails when people feel comfortable; leaders must surface a genuine, compelling reason to act now, not manufactured panic.

  2. Guiding coalition

    A single leader can't drive major change alone, it requires a cross-functional team with enough power, credibility, and trust to lead it.

  3. Strategic vision

    A clear, simple picture of how the future will differ from the present gives people something concrete to move toward and evaluate decisions against.

  4. Volunteer army

    Broad-based buy-in, a large number of people who genuinely want the change, not just comply with it, is what gives transformation its momentum.

  5. Removing barriers

    Structures, incentives, or managers that block the new direction have to be actively dismantled, not just tolerated alongside the old way.

  6. Short-term wins

    Visible, unambiguous early successes build credibility and quiet skeptics, which is why manufacturing and celebrating them deliberately matters.

Try it this week

  1. Write a one-paragraph case for urgency for a change you're trying to drive, using a concrete fact or comparison rather than abstract language.
  2. List the 3-5 people whose support you'd need on a guiding coalition for your current change effort, and note what each currently thinks.
  3. Identify one structural or process barrier actively blocking your team's new direction and name what removing it would require.
  4. Plan one short-term win you could generate and publicize within 60-90 days of starting a change effort.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Executives and project leaders driving formal, large-scale organizational change
  • Anyone who has seen a change initiative stall and wants to diagnose why

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting quick tactical tips for small-team or personal change, the book is written at enterprise-transformation scale
  • Those wanting the emotional and storytelling companion, Kotter's own follow-up The Heart of Change fits that better
