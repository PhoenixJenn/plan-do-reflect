Crystal Clear Communication

How to Explain Anything Clearly in Speech or Writing

by Gary S Goodman

2014 · 4h 18m audiobook · ★ 4.1 Audible

The big idea

Clear communication, in both speaking and writing, is a learnable, formula-driven skill, and eliminating ambiguity between you and your reader or listener is what makes messages land, persuade, and stick.

The PEP formula
A structuring formula Goodman describes as a method to get your point across clearly, every time, used to organize a message so the point is unmistakable rather than buried.

One program in Goodman's Nightingale-Conant audio catalog, built for listening in short, practical segments rather than as a traditional print book; the exact expansion of PEP couldn't be independently verified beyond the publisher's own description.

Key ideas

  1. Communication is a skill, not a gift

    Goodman frames clarity as trainable technique rather than natural talent.

  2. The three Ts

    Help you appeal to any audience by tailoring your message to who's actually listening.

  3. A four-step sales method

    Offered for persuading others, applicable to products, services, or ideas, not just formal sales.

  4. Breaking through writer's block

    Five specific strategies are given, treating it as a solvable, tactical problem.

  5. Sizing up your audience

    Quieting your thoughts and assessing who's listening before you speak or write is framed as essential prep, not optional.

  6. Stage fright, addressed directly

    Gets practical treatment alongside the writing-focused material, since the program covers both speech and text.

Try it this week

  1. Before your next important email, write one sentence stating your single point, then build the email around only that.
  2. Pick one piece of stalled writing and try writing the ending first, or writing it as if explaining to a friend out loud.
  3. Before your next presentation or difficult conversation, spend two minutes explicitly sizing up the audience.
  4. Apply the four-step sales approach to something non-sales, like pitching an idea to your team.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • People who want quick, audio-format tactics for business writing and speaking
  • Salespeople and presenters wanting a persuasion structure

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting a print book to flip through, since it's an audio program
  • Those seeking deep theory rather than quick applied formulas
