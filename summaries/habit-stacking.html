Habit Stacking

127 Small Changes to Improve Your Health, Wealth, and Happiness

by S.J. Scott

2017 · 6h 33m audiobook · ★ 4 Audible

The big idea

You can transform your life without relying on willpower by stacking a sequence of small habits, each five minutes or less, onto one existing trigger, gradually building a 15 to 30 minute automatic routine rather than attempting one dramatic change.

The habit stacking formula
One trigger, plus several small habits of five minutes or less, run in the same order daily until automatic, then scaled up over time.

Scott's version chains several small habits into one checklist-driven routine, not a single one-line swap of old habit for new.

Key ideas

  1. Three types of habits

    Keystone habits are foundational with ripple effects, like exercise. Support habits reinforce a keystone habit. Elephant habits are large goals broken into incremental steps.

  2. 127 habits across seven life categories

    Concrete habit examples organized across career, finance, health, organizing, relationships, leisure, and spirituality.

  3. Checklists over motivation

    A written daily checklist for your stacked routine removes decision fatigue and reliance on memory or willpower.

  4. Most important tasks

    Identify your top three priority tasks the evening before, so they get done regardless of what else happens that day.

  5. Gradual scaling

    Start with a small stack of a few minutes and only add new habits once the current stack is automatic, working up to a 30-minute routine.

  6. Restart without guilt

    Missed days are treated as normal. The book encourages resuming the stack immediately rather than abandoning it.

Try it this week

  1. Pick one existing daily trigger, like making coffee, and attach one new five-minute habit to it this week.
  2. Write tomorrow's three most important tasks tonight, before bed.
  3. Build a simple written checklist for a two or three habit starter stack and run it for seven straight days.
  4. If you miss a day, restart the next day rather than quitting the stack entirely.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • People overwhelmed trying to change many things at once who want a concrete, checklist-driven method
  • Readers who like example-heavy, highly practical books

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting the deeper psychology or neuroscience behind habit formation; Atomic Habits covers that ground more rigorously
  • Anyone wanting one unifying framework rather than a long catalog of discrete habit ideas
