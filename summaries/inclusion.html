Inclusion

Diversity, the New Workplace & the Will to Change

by Jennifer Brown

2017 · 7h 1m audiobook · ★ 4.4 Audible

The big idea

Building an inclusive workplace is a business imperative, not just a moral one; organizations that shift from command-and-control management to genuinely welcoming and valuing diverse talent become more resilient, innovative, and better positioned for the future of work.

The Diversity & Inclusion Self-Assessment
The book includes a self-assessment tool that helps readers evaluate their own organization's, and their own, inclusive leadership capabilities against time-tested strategies drawn from Fortune 500 implementations. It's designed as a starting diagnostic, helping a leader or team identify where they currently stand before applying the book's broader strategies for change.

none

Key ideas

  1. Diversity is defined broadly in the book

    Spanning identity, background, thought, style, approach, and experience, not just legally protected categories, widening what inclusion work actually covers.

  2. Traditional command-and-control management styles actively work against inclusion

    Because they concentrate voice and decision-making in a few people rather than drawing on the whole team.

  3. Diverse workforces are framed as more resilient, not just more innovative

    The argument is tied to organizational survival and adaptability, not only to creativity metrics.

  4. Genuine inclusion requires moving past aspirational statements and diversity goals

    Into actual behavior change and implementation.

  5. There's a reciprocal benefit to inclusion work

    As leaders make space for other people's identities and stories, they often uncover and better understand their own.

  6. The book draws on time-tested strategies already implemented at Fortune 500 companies

    Positioning itself as practically grounded rather than purely theoretical.

Try it this week

  1. Take the book's self-assessment, or write your own honest version, of your inclusive leadership capabilities before starting any new diversity initiative.
  2. Identify one recurring decision in your team where a command-and-control habit could be replaced with an actual group discussion this week.
  3. In one upcoming meeting, track who speaks and for how long, then use that to actively invite input from those who spoke least.
  4. Write down one goal your organization has stated about diversity, then identify a single concrete behavior change needed to move it from aspiration to practice.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Executives and HR leaders looking for a business-case-driven argument for inclusion they can bring to leadership
  • Readers newer to DEI work who want foundational grounding before moving to more tactical books

Less useful if

  • Readers who already know the business case for diversity and want granular, step-by-step tactics
  • Anyone looking for the most current statistics, since some data points reflect the book's original mid-2010s publication
