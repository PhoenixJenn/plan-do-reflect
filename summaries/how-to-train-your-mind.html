How to Train Your Mind

Exploring the Productivity Benefits of Meditation

by Chris Bailey

2021 · 3h 18m audiobook · ★ 4.6 Audible

The big idea

Meditation is presented here as a practical productivity tool rather than a spiritual practice. Training your attention in short daily sessions is meant to leave you calmer, clearer, and less prone to procrastination.

The headline claim
One minute of meditation can earn back about nine minutes of your day.

This ratio is Bailey's own estimate, based on his experiments and reported experience. Treat it as a motivating idea rather than an established finding.

Key ideas

  1. Attention is trainable

    The core practice is one-pointed attention, usually on the breath. Each time you notice your mind wandering and return, you are doing a repetition.

  2. A quieter mind works better

    Bailey argues that meditation reduces the constant stimulation our minds seek, which helps with clearer thinking and less avoidance of hard tasks.

  3. Start very small

    Short sessions are enough to begin, and the emphasis is on consistency over length. Habit-building tactics are part of the program.

  4. Practical rather than spiritual

    The book deliberately frames meditation around work and life outcomes, which makes it an easy entry for skeptics.

  5. It matters most when you are busiest

    Bailey suggests practice pays off most during stressful periods, which is also when people tend to skip it.

Try it this week

  1. Set a timer for five minutes tomorrow morning and focus on your breathing. When your mind wanders, note it and return.
  2. Attach the practice to something you already do, such as right after coffee.
  3. Keep a two-line log for a week: how long you sat and how focused you felt afterward.
  4. Decide after a week whether the effect on your focus justifies extending the time.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Skeptics who want a no-mysticism introduction to meditation
  • Busy people who need a short, tactical starting point
  • Readers who liked Bailey's productivity writing

Less useful if

  • Readers looking for a deep meditation tradition or teacher-led practice
  • Anyone who wants controlled evidence. The productivity numbers come from personal experiments and self-report
