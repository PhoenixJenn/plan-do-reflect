41 Self-Discipline Habits

For Slackers, Avoiders, & Couch Potatoes

by Peter Hollins

2021 · 3h 23m audiobook · ★ 4.6 Audible

The big idea

Self-discipline isn't one grand willpower marathon, it's a toolbox of small, concrete techniques spanning habits, mindset, mindfulness, and goal-setting that let procrastinators build momentum and close the gap between intention and action.

The two-day rule
Never let yourself skip a desired habit or action two days in a row. Miss one day, and get back to it the next, no exceptions.

A forgiveness-built-in consistency rule: the goal is preventing one slip from becoming a collapse, not demanding perfection.

Key ideas

  1. A toolbox, not a grind

    The book rejects grinding it out through sheer endurance, framing discipline as a set of learnable, low-effort tools rather than raw willpower.

  2. Small-start momentum

    The opening chapters focus on tiny first steps, like starting a new habit on a specific day, to build momentum before tackling bigger goals.

  3. Body and mind come first

    A dedicated chapter treats physical and emotional basics, energy, stress, and rest, as prerequisites discipline can't be built without.

  4. Grounding under stress

    A sensory grounding exercise, naming things you see, hear, touch, smell, and taste, for managing the anxiety that derails follow-through.

  5. Gradual habit replacement

    Swap bad habits for good ones incrementally rather than trying to eliminate them outright.

  6. Vision-driven goal work

    The closing chapters connect daily habits back to explicitly written goals, rather than treating habits as ends in themselves.

Try it this week

  1. Pick one habit you keep abandoning and apply the two-day rule to it for a week.
  2. Use a 5-4-3-2-1 senses grounding technique the next time stress tempts you to quit.
  3. Write down one specific goal by hand and put it somewhere visible.
  4. Start a new habit on a specific day, rather than whenever, and commit to that date.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Readers who want a grab-bag menu of quick, discrete tactics
  • Chronic procrastinators who need something to try today, not a theory to study

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting one unified explanatory framework for why they lack discipline; that's closer to the author's companion book, The Power of Self-Discipline
  • Can read as a loosely connected list rather than a progressive program
