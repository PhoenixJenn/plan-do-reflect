TED Talks

The Official TED Guide to Public Speaking

by Chris Anderson

2016 · 7h 31m audiobook · ★ 4.5 Audible

The big idea

TED curator Chris Anderson argues that presentation literacy is a core skill for our era, and that any talk succeeds or fails based on whether it builds a single clear throughline of an idea worth spreading, delivered with authentic human connection.

The throughline
Distill the entire talk into a single connecting thread of no more than about 15 words, a rope that every story, fact, and example in the talk should visibly hang from, so the audience never loses the thread.

Anderson's own coinage as TED's longtime curator, drawn from watching thousands of TED talks succeed or fail on structure.

Key ideas

  1. Presentation literacy

    Public speaking is a learnable, teachable skill with outsized career and civic impact, not a fixed talent.

  2. Five tools for building a talk

    Connection, Narration, Explanation, Persuasion, and Revelation are the core techniques for developing and delivering an idea.

  3. Connect before you present

    Open by establishing trust and common ground with the audience before diving into content.

  4. Explain from the known to the unknown

    Layer complex ideas step by step, building from what the audience already understands.

  5. Persuade by demolishing objections first

    Before presenting your case, address and dismantle the audience's likely counterarguments.

  6. Visuals should support, not star

    Use high-resolution images, minimal text, and large single typefaces so slides amplify rather than compete with the speaker.

Try it this week

  1. Write your talk's throughline in 15 words or fewer before writing anything else.
  2. Pick one of the five tools, like Narration, and rebuild your opening around it.
  3. Cut every slide's text down to a single phrase or number, replacing paragraphs with one strong image.
  4. Script and memorize just your first and last two sentences, even if the middle stays loose.

Is it for you?
