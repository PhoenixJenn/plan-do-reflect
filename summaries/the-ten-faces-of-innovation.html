The Ten Faces of Innovation

by Tom Kelley, Jonathan Littman

2006 · 8h 49m audiobook · ★ 4 Audible

The big idea

Organizations kill innovation when they let devil's-advocate skepticism dominate; instead, teams should deliberately cultivate ten distinct innovation personas, spanning learning, organizing, and building roles, that individuals can rotate through to keep creative momentum alive.

The ten personas framework
Kelley groups ten roles into three categories: learning personas, the Anthropologist, Experimenter, and Cross-Pollinator, who gather fresh input; organizing personas, the Hurdler, Collaborator, and Director, who navigate people and obstacles; and building personas, the Experience Architect, Set Designer, Storyteller, and Caregiver, who turn insight into tangible innovation. A team doesn't need ten separate people, individuals can adopt different personas depending on the challenge at hand.

The book draws its examples heavily from Kelley's own firm, IDEO, and client companies, so the framework reflects a design-consultancy lens more than a universal management theory.

Key ideas

  1. The Anthropologist

    Observes how real people actually use products and services in context, surfacing unmet needs that surveys and focus groups miss.

  2. The Experimenter

    Learns through rapid, hands-on prototyping and treats early failures as cheap information rather than setbacks.

  3. The Cross-Pollinator

    Deliberately imports ideas and metaphors from other industries or cultures to spark novel solutions in their own domain.

  4. The Hurdler

    Faces unprecedented obstacles with resourceful, optimistic problem-solving rather than getting stuck waiting for permission or perfect conditions.

  5. The Collaborator

    Breaks down organizational silos by assembling multidisciplinary teams and empowering members to contribute outside their usual lane.

  6. The Storyteller and Experience Architect

    Build compelling narratives and designed, memorable end-to-end experiences that make innovations resonate emotionally, not just functionally.

Try it this week

  1. Identify which of the ten personas comes most naturally to you, and deliberately practice the persona you use least in your next project meeting.
  2. Spend 30 minutes this week directly observing how someone actually uses a product or process you're responsible for, taking notes like an Anthropologist.
  3. Prototype one small, rough version of an idea you've been overthinking, purely to get real reaction from a colleague or customer.
  4. Pull one person outside your usual collaborators into a current project for a single working session.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Team leads and innovation managers wanting a shared vocabulary for different creative roles on a team
  • Design and product teams looking to diagnose which capabilities are missing from a stalled project

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting quantitative or financial innovation metrics rather than role-based, qualitative guidance
  • Very small teams or solo founders who can't realistically distribute ten distinct roles
