Building a StoryBrand

Clarify Your Message So Customers Will Listen

by Donald Miller

4h 56m audiobook

The big idea

Most marketing fails because it makes the brand the hero of the story. Miller argues brands should instead cast the customer as the hero and position themselves as the guide who helps that hero solve a problem and win.

The SB7 framework
A seven-part story structure for any marketing message: a Hero, the customer, has a Problem, meets a Guide, the brand, who gives them a Plan, and calls them to Action, that helps them avoid failure and ends in success.

Miller illustrates the guide role with the mentor archetype, like Mr. Miyagi or Yoda, mentors who empower the hero rather than overshadow them; meant to be filled out as a one-page BrandScript.

Key ideas

  1. Customers are the hero, not your brand

    Messaging that centers the company loses customers who are looking for themselves in the story.

  2. Confusion kills conversion

    If customers have to work to understand what you offer or how it helps them, they tune out; clarity beats cleverness.

  3. Three layers of problem

    Every brand should identify the customer's external, internal, and philosophical problem, since most companies only address the surface-level one.

  4. Guide, not hero

    A brand becomes trustworthy by showing empathy and authority rather than showcasing the company's own story.

  5. A clear plan

    A simple plan, often 3 steps, removes the customer's fear of commitment or complexity.

  6. Direct, repeated calls to action

    Vague, buried CTAs are a common reason marketing underperforms.

Try it this week

  1. Rewrite your website's homepage headline so the customer, not your product, is the subject of the sentence.
  2. List your customer's external problem, then push one level deeper and name the internal, emotional problem it creates.
  3. Draft a 3-step plan a new customer would follow after saying yes to your product or offer.
  4. Find your strongest call-to-action line and make sure it appears in at least two places a customer would naturally look.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Marketers and business owners struggling to explain what they do
  • Bloggers and consultants clarifying their value proposition

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting SEO or paid-ads tactics rather than message clarity
  • Those looking for narrative craft for fiction rather than sales copy
