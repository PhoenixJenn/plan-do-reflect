Actions Speak Louder

A Step-by-Step Guide to Becoming an Inclusive Workplace

by Deanna Singh

2022 · 6h 20m audiobook · ★ 5 Audible

The big idea

Building an inclusive workplace is not about good intentions, mission statements, or one-off listening sessions; it requires organizations to redesign concrete systems, recruitment, hiring, retention, and culture, so equity is built into how decisions actually get made.

The Four-Area DEI Audit
Singh structures the book's action steps around four organizational areas where exclusion typically hides: recruitment, hiring, retention, and day-to-day culture. Readers are guided to examine each area individually and identify specific, fixable practices reinforcing exclusion rather than treating diversity as one abstract problem.

none

Key ideas

  1. Diversity, equity, and inclusion form a progression, not synonyms

    Diversity brings different perspectives into the room, equity builds the systems that let them stay, and inclusion is reached only when those employees can actually help shape decisions.

  2. Passive assumptions are themselves a form of exclusion

    Recruitment has to be actively redesigned to reach different candidate pools rather than assuming underrepresented candidates just don't apply here.

  3. Interview processes often default to rewarding people who resemble existing employees

    Structured, standardized interview questions are offered as a countermeasure to that status-quo bias.

  4. Retention needs to be deliberately designed around underrepresented employees' actual needs

    Since default systems tend to reproduce existing patterns of who stays and who leaves.

  5. Inclusive meetings depend on deliberately inviting dissent

    And amplifying quieter or marginalized voices, not just inviting diverse people into the room and hoping they speak up.

  6. The book uses a marathon-training analogy

    Lasting inclusion needs formal plans, structures, and support systems, not just individual willpower or good intentions.

Try it this week

  1. This week, review the wording of your team's most recent job posting for language or channels that may be narrowing the applicant pool.
  2. In your next round of interviews, write out identical, structured questions in advance for every candidate rather than improvising based on the conversation.
  3. In your next team meeting, name one colleague whose perspective is usually underrepresented and directly ask for their view before the group moves to a decision.
  4. Ask one underrepresented colleague this week, privately, what would most help them bring their full perspective to the team, and act on one piece of that feedback.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • Managers and DEI practitioners who want a practical, step-by-step workbook rather than a theory-first book
  • Teams ready to audit specific practices rather than debate concepts

Less useful if

  • Readers looking for a research-heavy or academic treatment of workplace inclusion
  • Organizations at a very early why-does-this-matter stage, since the book assumes buy-in and moves quickly into implementation
