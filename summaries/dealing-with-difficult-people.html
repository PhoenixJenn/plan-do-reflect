Dealing with Difficult People

HBR Emotional Intelligence Series

by Harvard Business Review

2022 · 1h 47m audiobook · ★ 4.3 Audible

The big idea

This curated collection of Harvard Business Review articles argues that the key to handling difficult colleagues is managing emotions, both the other person's and your own, rather than trying to win the interaction.

Hot conflict vs. cold conflict
The collection distinguishes hot conflicts, visible anger, tension, sniping, from cold conflicts, withdrawal, silent resentment, passive aggression, and argues each requires a different de-escalation approach: hot conflict needs emotional cooling before any content discussion, cold conflict needs surfacing the unspoken issue.

One of several distinct tools in the book rather than a single unifying model, since it's an anthology of separate articles, not a single-author framework.

Key ideas

  1. Own your contribution

    Before labeling someone difficult, examine how your own behavior or expectations feed the friction.

  2. Manage your own emotions first

    Techniques for staying composed, like naming your emotional state and pausing, prevent you from escalating a tense exchange.

  3. Passive-aggressive colleagues

    Practical scripts for naming indirect hostility without triggering more defensiveness.

  4. The chronically stressed coworker

    Advice on adjusting your approach with colleagues whose stress-reactivity colors every interaction.

  5. The everything-is-urgent colleague

    Strategies for negotiating priorities with someone who treats all requests as emergencies.

  6. Managing up when a boss is difficult

    Tactics for handling a supervisor's difficult behavior without damaging the relationship or your position.

Try it this week

  1. Identify one recurring conflict and label it hot or cold, then try the matching de-escalation tactic this week.
  2. Before your next tense exchange, name your own emotional state silently before responding.
  3. Write down one way you may be contributing to a difficult dynamic with a specific coworker.
  4. Try one scripted phrase from the passive-aggression material next time someone gives you a backhanded comment.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • People new to workplace conflict skills wanting a fast, practical primer
  • Readers who prefer short, bite-sized articles over a single long book

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting one cohesive framework rather than several separate essays
  • Readers facing deeply entrenched or complex conflicts, which reviewers note it breezes through
