Leadership Skills for Successful Managers

Proven Strategies for Motivating Teams and Achieving Your Goals

by Ron Lagrone

2023 · 5h 5m audiobook · ★ 5 Audible

The big idea

Effective leadership comes from treating employees as whole people -- acting as a mentor, coach, and cheerleader in addition to a boss -- and building trust through transparency and open dialogue rather than authority alone.

none identifiable
Available descriptions of the book describe general practices around relationship-building, coaching, delegation, and reducing turnover, but no independent review or excerpt identifies a specific named model or step-by-step framework comparable to mainstream management books. The book is organized into eight chapters covering relationship-building, communication-based conflict resolution, and building employee confidence, presented as advice and anecdotes drawn from the author's decades running small businesses.

This is a self-published title (the author is his own publisher) written with a ghostwriter through a self-publishing course; it has no mainstream publisher and no notable independent reviews beyond a handful of reader ratings and one local newspaper feature -- treat any 'framework' claim about this book with caution.

Key ideas

  1. Manager as multiple roles

    The book's central pitch is that a good manager operates as boss, mentor, cheerleader, and coach simultaneously, not just a task-assigner.

  2. Trust through transparency

    Lagrone emphasizes open, honest dialogue with employees as the foundation for reducing turnover and building durable working relationships.

  3. Confidence-building as a leadership act

    A recurring theme is that leadership means building confidence between people, positioning employee self-belief as a manager's direct responsibility.

  4. Lessons from small-business ownership

    The content is drawn from Lagrone's experience founding and running a home-building company, a home-inspection business, and a vending machine company.

Try it this week

  1. Have one direct, transparent conversation this week with a struggling employee about a workplace issue you'd normally soften or avoid.
  2. Identify one employee whose confidence you could deliberately build through a specific vote of trust rather than general praise.

Is it for you?

Best for

  • First-time small-business owner-operators looking for accessible, anecdote-driven advice from someone who built and ran hands-on businesses.
  • Readers who prefer a short, informal, story-based read over a research-backed management text.

Less useful if

  • Readers wanting rigorously tested frameworks, case studies, or research citations.
  • Managers at larger or more complex organizations, since the content draws narrowly from one person's small-business experience.
