Leadership Tenets

Short, memorable principles for how you lead when nothing else spells it out — not rules, just the reflex you fall back on under pressure.

Try It

Team's Tenets guide how a group decides when a process doesn't spell out the next step. Leadership Tenets work the same way, one level up — they're about who you are as a leader, not what a team is optimizing for this quarter. The best ones are short enough to say from memory, specific enough to actually change a decision in the moment, and few enough that you can hold all of them at once — most people land somewhere around four to six. And like Team's, "unless you know better ones" keeps them a living guide instead of a fixed law.

Examples

Two real sets, four tenets each, to show the range a set can take — one built around energy and presence, the other around growth and craft.

Set 1 — Energy & Presence

1

Lead by Example — your actions speak louder than words. Demonstrate excellence and show others what's possible through your own behavior.

2

Be Optimistic — enthusiasm is infectious and energizes your team. A positive attitude fosters a productive environment; negativity can be detrimental.

3

Embrace Change — when faced with obstacles, find a way forward rather than resisting. Adopt a mindset of adaptability; success often requires the ability to pivot when necessary.

4

Connect and Communicate — human connection is the foundation for success. Get to know your colleagues, their goals, and their achievements. Build a strong network by regularly attending events, reaching out to new people, and facilitating connections for others — being able to connect the dots is a key to innovation.

Set 2 — Growth & Craft

1

First, Seek to Understand — not all problems require solutions. Make sure you fully understand an issue before deciding whether it warrants action; time is a precious resource, use it wisely.

2

Plan. Do. Reflect. — yes, this is also where this site's name comes from.

  • Set ambitious, visionary goals.
  • Maintain laser focus and discipline — consistency is what gets you there, and small actions compound over time.
  • Regularly ask: "Is what I'm doing today bringing me closer to my long-term objectives?" Document progress, reflect often, and adjust as needed.
3

Learn. Do. Teach.

  • Embrace continuous learning.
  • Werner Vogels opened his 2019 AWS re:Invent keynote with "there's no compression algorithm for experience." True — but there are ways to accelerate how fast you get it. Progress takes fresh approaches and new tools, so stay curious, experiment, and keep seeking out new perspectives and methods; innovation follows.
  • Share what you discover — teaching can be as rewarding as doing.
4

Dare to Be Different — your personal brand matters. Aim to create unmatched, exceptional experiences. Pay attention to detail, and know when it's appropriate to break the rules; innovation often comes from challenging the status quo.

Watch-out

A tenet that sounds good in a deck but has never once changed what you actually did in the room isn't a tenet — it's wall art. Test yours: can you point to a real moment where one of them tipped a decision, not just a quality you'd like people to associate with you?

Try It

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, or whatever AI tool you use:

Help me draft 4-6 Leadership Tenets — short, memorable principles for how I lead when nothing else spells it out. Ask me about moments I was proud of how I led and moments I wasn't, what I actually fall back on under pressure, and what I want people who've worked for me to say about me years later. Push back if an answer is too generic to actually change a decision, and help me tighten each one down to a short, sayable name plus one or two sentences on why it matters.
