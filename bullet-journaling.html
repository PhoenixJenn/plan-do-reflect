Bullet Journaling

One concrete mechanism for running the whole cycle — not just knowing about it.

Reflection Practice 3 core mechanics Try It

Why It Works

Knowing the cycle isn't the same as running it. Plan-Do-Reflect only sticks if you've got somewhere to actually put it — a place to write down what you're aiming for, log what you actually did, and look back before you plan again. Without a mechanism like that, "reflect" stays a vague intention instead of something that happens on a schedule.

Bullet Journaling — Ryder Carroll's system of rapid logging in brief daily entries, migrated forward instead of left to pile up in old notebooks — is one concrete way to build that mechanism. Its own structure maps onto the cycle almost exactly:

  • Monthly Log — a running month-at-a-glance list of tasks and events, set up before the month starts. This is your Plan.
  • Daily Log — quick, same-day notes on what you actually did, not what you meant to do. This is your Do.
  • Migration — at the end of each month, decide what to carry forward, what to schedule, and what to drop. This is your Reflect.

The Core Mechanics

Three habits that make the system run. Learn these once and every log, no matter the topic, works the same way.

1

Rapid Logging

The notation that makes daily entries fast enough to actually keep up: short, single-line entries instead of full sentences, each marked with a symbol so you can scan a page at a glance.

  • • Task — something to do. Mark it x when done, > when migrated forward.
  • ○ Event — something that happened, or will, at a specific time.
  • – Note — a fact, idea, or observation worth keeping, with nothing to act on.
  • * and ! — optional signifiers for priority and inspiration, added in front of any entry.
2

Collections

Any group of entries on the same topic, gathered onto their own page instead of scattered across daily logs. The Monthly Log is the collection everyone starts with, but the same idea works for anything recurring — a running list of 1:1 notes, a project tracker, books to read.

3

Migration

The habit that keeps the whole system honest: at the end of each month, go through every open task and ask, for each one, whether it still matters. Do it now, schedule it for later, move it to a collection, or cross it out entirely. A task that isn't worth rewriting by hand usually wasn't worth keeping — migration is what keeps a log from turning into a graveyard of things you never actually meant to finish.
