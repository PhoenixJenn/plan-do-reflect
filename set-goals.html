SMART Goals

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Individual by nature — less a framework than a filter you run your own goal through before committing time to it, and a vague intention ("get better at this") turns into something you can actually plan around and know when you've hit.

Examples

  • "Follow a weekly training plan so I can complete a half marathon in 6 months."
  • "Study 4 hours per week on Fridays so I can pass my AWS Certification exam in 3 months."
2

OKRs (Objectives & Key Results)

Native habitat is org or team alignment — a small set of shared objectives with measurable key results everyone can see progress against. The Objective is the what — the qualitative direction you're headed. The Key Results are the how — the measurable proof you actually got there. Worth borrowing at the individual level too: pick 1–3 personal objectives and 3–5 measurable key results per objective, and a goal stops being a wish the moment it has a number attached. If SMART Goals is how you check one goal on your own, OKRs is how a goal cascades — from the org's, to the team's, to yours.

The 5 Core Principles of OKRs:

  • Focus — limits top priorities to a small number of objectives (usually 3), each with 3–5 key results, so teams don't get distracted by lesser tasks.
  • Alignment — connects individual and team goals upward to company-wide priorities, so everyone's pulling in the same direction.
  • Commitment — builds shared agreement and ownership across teams for what actually needs to happen.
  • Tracking — uses measurable data to check progress and evaluate success continuously, not just at the end.
  • Stretching — encourages ambitious "moonshot" goals that push teams past their comfort zone to drive real innovation.
Example

Objective: Make our onboarding experience genuinely great.

Key Results:

  • Cut time-to-first-commit from 10 days to 4
  • New-hire satisfaction survey hits 9/10
  • 90% of new hires ship a real feature in their first two weeks

SMART Goals vs. OKRs

Both live on this page for a reason — they're not competing frameworks, they answer different questions.

SMART GoalsOKRs
StructureA set of rules for writing one goal wellA qualitative Objective, plus 3–5 measurable Key Results
TargetMeant to be 100% achievableStretch goals — hitting 70–80% is often viewed as a strong win
ScopeIndividual tasks or short-term projectsScales across teams and orgs, linking daily work to company strategy
Tied toIndividual pay and performance reviewsBusiness value and strategic priorities — deliberately kept separate from personal compensation, which is what makes high-risk, high-reward targets safe to set
Use it whenYou need clear direction for an individual task, personal development, or a routine processYou want cross-functional alignment, company-wide transparency, and rapid growth over a quarterly cycle

Try It

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, or whatever AI tool you use:

I want to create a SMART goal. Ask me what I'm actually trying to achieve, then help me turn it into a SMART goal — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. If it's something bigger that spans a few months, also help me draft it as an Objective with 3 measurable Key Results.
