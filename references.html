References & Further Reading

The ideas behind this site aren't new. They're pulled together from research and writing on reflection, learning, and sustained growth. Here's where to go deeper — organized by the homepage section each set of ideas supports, plus a section per stage as those pages fill in.

Plan — Goal Setting & Priorities

Further reading

  • Gary Keller & Jay Papasan, The ONE Thing (2013). Extraordinary results come from extreme focus — doing less by narrowing to the single most important task instead of spreading across many.

Do — Focus & Deep Work

Further reading

  • Cal Newport, Deep Work (2016). The source for the 4 Rules of Deep Work and the Shutdown Ritual — the case for scheduled, undistracted focus as a rare and valuable skill worth protecting deliberately.

Do — Time Management

Further reading

  • David Allen, Getting Things Done (2001). The source for the 2-Minute Rule — a full system for capturing everything so your mind doesn't have to hold it.
  • Brian Tracy, Eat That Frog! (2001). Do the hardest, most important task first, before anything else gets a chance to eat the day instead.
  • Oliver Burkeman, Four Thousand Weeks (2021). A corrective to productivity culture itself — you will never get everything done, and that's the actual starting point, not a problem to solve.
  • Laura Vanderkam, 168 Hours (2010). Reframes "I don't have time" as a prioritization problem, not a math problem — everyone gets the same 168 hours a week.
  • Damon Zahariades, The Art of Saying No (2017). Actionable steps to stop people-pleasing and protect your schedule — pairs directly with the Boundaries & Saying No group above.
  • Susan Newman, The Book of No (2005). Practical scripts for turning down requests from colleagues, friends, and family.

Do — Habit-Building

Further reading

  • James Clear, Atomic Habits (2018). The source for the 4 Laws of Behavior Change and habit stacking — small, systematic changes compound into remarkably different outcomes over time.
  • Stephen Covey, The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People (1989). A broader personal-effectiveness philosophy rather than habit-formation mechanics — its ideas show up scattered across this site (Begin with the End in Mind, Put First Things First) more than on this page specifically.

Do — Motivation & Procrastination

Further reading

  • Gabriele Oettingen, Rethinking Positive Thinking (2014). The research behind the WOOP Method — positive thinking alone underperforms positive thinking paired with a real obstacle and a plan.

Team — Project & Program Management

Further reading

  • Colin Powell, It Worked for Me: In Life and Leadership (2012). Source of "Bad news isn't wine. It doesn't improve with age," quoted on the Status Report Template — the case for flagging at risk and off track honestly instead of letting a red status quietly age into a crisis.

Team — Product Management

Further reading

  • Simon Sinek, Start with Why (2009). The source for the Strategy-is-the-why framing on Product Vision & Strategy — people follow the reason behind a plan more than the plan itself.
  • Richard Rumelt, Good Strategy Bad Strategy (2011). The sharpest explanation of why strategy is hard: most of what gets called strategy is really just a goal or vision statement in disguise. Real strategy is a diagnosis, a guiding policy, and coherent action.
  • Colin Bryar & Bill Carr, Working Backwards: Insights, Stories, and Secrets from Inside Amazon (2021). Written by two former Amazon executives who helped build the process; the definitive source for the PR/FAQ method on the Working Backwards Plan section — why the press release is dated as if the product already shipped and succeeded, not written on day one.

Why it matters

References

  • Di Stefano, Gino, Pisano & Staats, "The Power of Reflection at Work," Harvard Business Review (2014)

Further reading

  • John Dewey, Experience and Education (1938). The foundational case that we don't learn from experience alone, we learn from reflecting on it.
  • Donald A. Schön, The Reflective Practitioner: How Professionals Think in Action (1983). A classic on reflection-in-action across professions.

What it is

References

  • "Kolb's Learning Styles & Experiential Learning Cycle," Simply Psychology

Further reading

How this site supports it

Further reading

  • Tiago Forte, Building a Second Brain (2022). Organizing what you learn so it compounds instead of evaporating.
  • James Clear, Atomic Habits (2018). Small systems and cues that make a practice stick.

When to use it

Further reading

  • Dan Sullivan & Dr. Benjamin Hardy, The Gap and the Gain (2021). Measuring progress by looking back at how far you've come, not just at how far you have to go.
  • Bill Burnett & Dave Evans, Designing Your Life (2016). Treating your career and life as something you periodically redesign, not something you set once.
