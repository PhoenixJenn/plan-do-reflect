Time Management

Protecting the time you already decided mattered. A small toolkit of frameworks, not a productivity personality.

The Framework 5 frameworks

Why It Matters

You can have the clearest plan in the world and still lose the day to whatever shows up loudest. Time management isn't about cramming more in — it's about protecting space for the work that actually moves things forward, so Planning and Reflecting have somewhere to land. Without it, the Plan-Do-Reflect cycle has no "Do" to speak of: just a plan that never got a turn and a reflection with nothing to look back on. Master a handful of reliable frameworks and time management stops being a personality trait and starts being a system you can run on a bad day, not just a good one.

Frameworks We Cover

Pulled from a "best of" pass across 28 source pins on this topic — these 5 are the ones with real depth behind them; everything else recurred only as a line item inside someone else's roundup.

1

The 80/20 Rule

Roughly 20% of your effort drives 80% of your results. The skill isn't working harder — it's finding your vital 20% and giving it your full attention.

2

Pomodoro Technique

Work in focused 25-minute sprints with 5-minute breaks. Simple enough to start today, structured enough to actually stick.

3

The 2-Minute Rule

If it takes less than two minutes, do it now — don't let it become a line on tomorrow's to-do list.

4

Time Blocking

Protect your calendar the way you'd protect a meeting with your most important client. Deep work needs a scheduled home, not just an intention.

5

Boundaries & Saying No

Techniques don't help if work has no edges. The actual phrases to use when you're at capacity — not just the idea that you should have limits.
