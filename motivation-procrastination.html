Motivation & Procrastination

For the days the system alone isn't enough — stuck on a specific goal, a specific task, or a specific excuse.

Self 1 framework Try It

Why It Matters

Habit-Building covers the system that makes discipline unnecessary most days. This page is for the days the system alone isn't enough — motivation isn't showing up, and you're stuck on something specific rather than everything at once.

"Procrastination isn't a time-management problem. It's an emotion-regulation problem."

— procrastination research, Dr. Timothy Pychyl

You're usually not procrastinating because you can't plan your day — you're avoiding the uncomfortable feeling attached to the task. Fix the feeling, or work around it, and the time-management problem mostly disappears on its own.

Frameworks We Cover

One tool for the obstacle you can see coming before you even start.

1

The WOOP Method

Developed by psychologist Gabriele Oettingen: positive thinking alone doesn't get you there — it takes picturing the outcome and naming the obstacle in advance, so you already have a plan when it shows up instead of getting derailed by it in the moment.

W

Wish

Pick a goal you genuinely care about — specific and challenging, but doable.

O

Outcome

Picture what success actually looks and feels like.

O

Obstacle

Name the real thing most likely to get in your way — usually something in you, not the world.

P

Plan

Decide now: "If [obstacle], then I will [action]."

W: run a 10K this spring
O: cross the finish line, feel proud
O: I skip runs when work runs late
P: if I miss one, I run at lunch instead

Tip — Common Motivation Killers

No Clear Goal. Fear of Failure. Comparison to Others. Perfectionism. Negative Self-Talk. Different root causes, same result — notice which one is actually running the show before you try to fix it with more willpower.

Try It

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, or whatever AI tool you use:

I'm stuck on something and my motivation isn't showing up. Ask me what I'm avoiding and why, then help me run it through the WOOP method — Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan — to build a specific if-then plan for the obstacle.
