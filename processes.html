Processes

Self-Management, Leading, and Managing are about how you operate. This is about how the work itself gets organized — the recurring rhythms, planning structures, and execution methods that hold regardless of who's running them.

Root category 8 categories

Ordered as an initiative's actual lifecycle rather than alphabetically: the ambient rhythm it happens inside, planning it, executing it, building it, and troubleshooting when it breaks. One category — Operational Process — doesn't fit that lifecycle at all, since it's a standing business function rather than something with a start and an end, so it's called out separately below.

An Initiative's Lifecycle

From the ambient rhythm everything else happens inside, through planning, execution, and what to do when it breaks.

Team Operating Rhythm & Cadence

Standups, retros, and status reporting — the ambient cycle everything else happens inside.

Research

Customer discovery, interviews, and Jobs to Be Done — building real understanding of the customer, not the fast intake version Product Management uses to get started.

Product Management

Product strategy, roadmapping, and prioritization — defining what to build and why, before Project & Program Management figures out how.

Project & Program Management

Project Charter, RACI/DACI, Critical Path, Risk Management, and more — planning a specific initiative before you run it.

Agile / Delivery Process

Scrum, Kanban, and sprint ceremonies — how the plan actually gets executed, cycle by cycle. Also home to Retrospectives, Post-Mortems, and Pre-Mortems.

Technical / Engineering Process

Design docs, code review, and technical design — how the thing itself gets built.

Quality & Problem-Solving

Root Cause Analysis, Lean, and Six Sigma — what to run when something breaks.

Standing Functions

Doesn't fit the initiative-lifecycle frame above — a function the business runs continuously, not a project with an end date.

Operational Process

Procurement & Sourcing — also referenced from Business Acumen, where it's framed around the spend itself rather than the process.
