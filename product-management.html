Product Management

Defining what to build and why, before Project & Program Management figures out how — product strategy, roadmapping, and prioritization.

Processes 5 frameworks · 1 built Try It

Click a section below to expand or collapse it.

A vision without a strategy is a poster. A strategy without a vision is a to-do list. Fill in these eight fields to connect the two, and revisit them whenever a roadmap decision needs a tiebreaker:

  • Vision Statement — one sentence, the future state you're aiming at (3-5 years out), not a list of things to ship.
  • Target Customer — who this is for, and just as important, who it's explicitly not for.
  • Problem / Opportunity — the unmet need in the market that justifies the vision.
  • Why Now — what's already true about the market that makes this winnable today, not five years ago or five years from now.
  • Strategic Bets — the 2-4 big choices this cycle that ladder up to the vision. A bet is something you could be wrong about — if it's not risky, it's not a bet, it's a feature.
  • Differentiation — why this, and not a competitor or the status quo.
  • Non-Goals — deliberately out of scope. Naming what you won't do protects the bets you did make.
  • Success Measure — how you'll know the strategy is working.

The most common failure isn't a bad vision statement — it's strategic bets that are actually just a feature list wearing a strategy costume. A real bet names a belief that could turn out to be wrong.

Example: iPod

Vision: Every song you own, in your pocket, always with you.

Target Customer: People who already own a real music collection and are tired of lugging around a CD binder — not casual listeners happy with the radio.

Problem / Opportunity: Existing MP3 players in 2001 held a dozen songs and took forever to load. Nobody had solved "my whole library, always with me."

Why Now: The Sony Walkman had already normalized wearing headphones and listening to music in public — the iPod didn't have to create that behavior, just make it better.

Strategic Bet: Bet on a tightly closed hardware-plus-iTunes system over an open, mix-and-match MP3 player market — control the whole experience end to end.

Differentiation: A 5GB hard drive, a scroll wheel you could operate one-handed, and FireWire sync fast enough to load 1,000 songs in under 10 minutes.

Non-Goals: Not a phone, not a video player, not a photo album — just music, done better than anyone else, at launch.

Success Measure: iPod becomes the default way people carry their music, not just another player on the shelf.

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, or whatever AI tool you use:

I'm defining product strategy for a product or feature area. Ask me who the customer is and what problem I'm solving first, then walk me through drafting each field one at a time: Vision Statement (one sentence, 3-5 years out), Target Customer (who it's for, and who it's explicitly not for), Problem/Opportunity, Why Now (what's already true about the market that makes this winnable today), Strategic Bets (2-4 big bets, not a feature list), Differentiation, Non-Goals, and Success Measure. Push back if a "bet" is really just a feature idea in disguise, if my vision statement reads like a to-do list instead of a future state, or if my "Why Now" is really just wishful thinking about timing rather than something already true in the market.
