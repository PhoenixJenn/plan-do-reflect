OKRs

Objectives & Key Results — how a goal cascades from the org's, to the team's, to yours.

Organization Plan Try It

Native habitat is org or team alignment — a small set of shared objectives with measurable key results everyone can see progress against. The Objective is the what — the qualitative direction you're headed. The Key Results are the how — the measurable proof you actually got there. Worth borrowing at the individual level too: pick 1–3 personal objectives and 3–5 measurable key results per objective, and a goal stops being a wish the moment it has a number attached. If SMART Goals is how you check one goal on your own, OKRs is how a goal cascades — from the org's, to the team's, to yours.

The 5 Core Principles of OKRs:

  • Focus — limits top priorities to a small number of objectives (usually 3), each with 3–5 key results, so teams don't get distracted by lesser tasks.
  • Alignment — connects individual and team goals upward to company-wide priorities, so everyone's pulling in the same direction.
  • Commitment — builds shared agreement and ownership across teams for what actually needs to happen.
  • Tracking — uses measurable data to check progress and evaluate success continuously, not just at the end.
  • Stretching — encourages ambitious "moonshot" goals that push teams past their comfort zone to drive real innovation.
Example

Objective: Make our onboarding experience genuinely great.

Key Results:

  • Cut time-to-first-commit from 10 days to 4
  • New-hire satisfaction survey hits 9/10
  • 90% of new hires ship a real feature in their first two weeks

OKRs vs. SMART Goals

Not competing frameworks — they answer different questions. See Set Goals for SMART's own write-up.

OKRsSMART Goals
StructureA qualitative Objective, plus 3–5 measurable Key ResultsA set of rules for writing one goal well
TargetStretch goals — hitting 70–80% is often viewed as a strong winMeant to be 100% achievable
ScopeScales across teams and orgs, linking daily work to company strategyIndividual tasks or short-term projects
Tied toBusiness value and strategic priorities — deliberately kept separate from personal compensation, which is what makes high-risk, high-reward targets safe to setIndividual pay and performance reviews
Use it whenYou want cross-functional alignment, company-wide transparency, and rapid growth over a quarterly cycleYou need clear direction for an individual task, personal development, or a routine process
