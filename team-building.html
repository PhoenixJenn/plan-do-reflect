Team Building

Not a Plan, Do, or Reflect step — building how a group of individuals actually works together as a team, which every one of those steps depends on.

Try It

This page will grow to cover more than one activity over time. Starting with the one every team eventually runs into: a color-coded personality assessment.

1

Team Color Assessments

A team color assessment — Insights Discovery, DISC, and Birkman are the three you'll run into most — categorizes workplace personalities and communication preferences into four primary color energies. None of them are a clinical diagnosis; think of them as a shared vocabulary for naming a style difference before it turns into a personal one.

Red — Action, Dominance & Results

Direct, competitive, and fast-paced. Focuses on "what next" and the bottom line, preferring quick decisions over long discussions.

Under stress: can turn demanding or overbearing.

Yellow — Enthusiasm, Influence & Energy

Social, optimistic, and expressive. Brings big-picture creativity and team engagement, though may lack detail or organization.

Under stress: can turn hasty or scattered.

Green — Stability, Empathy & Support

Patient, loyal, and relationship-focused. Values harmony, listening, and steady collaboration, but may resist sudden change.

Under stress: can turn stubborn or overly cautious.

Blue — Analysis, Precision & Logic

Cautious, systematic, and detail-oriented. Asks "why" and relies on data, structure, and thorough planning.

Under stress: can turn overly critical, reserved, or skeptical.

2

How Teams Use the Model

The point was never to put four stickers on four foreheads — it's a working tool for communication, planning, and conflict.

Communication

Adapt how you talk to each style: bullet points and facts for Red, deep data for Blue, energy and brainstorming space for Yellow, reassurance and time to process for Green.

Balanced Execution

A well-rounded team needs all four energies across a project's phases — Blue to plan, Green to build stakeholder buy-in, Red to execute, Yellow to inspire or pivot.

Conflict Resolution

Most behavioral friction traces back to opposing color preferences — a fast-moving Red clashing with a methodical Blue. Naming that reduces it to a style mismatch, not a personal one.

Try It

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, or whatever AI tool you use:

Help my team figure out which color energies (Red, Yellow, Green, Blue) we each lean toward and where our friction probably comes from. Ask me to describe 2-3 recent moments of team friction, then help me map them to color-style clashes and suggest one concrete adjustment each person could try.
