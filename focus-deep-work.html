Focus & Deep Work

Decide what matters enough to protect, before you decide how to spend it.

Self 2 frameworks Try It

Why It Matters

Time Management protects a block on the calendar. This is the step before that — deciding which kind of work actually deserves a block in the first place. Without it, Time Management just protects your attention for shallow work more efficiently, which isn't the point.

"Human beings, it seems, are at their best when immersed deeply in something challenging."

— Cal Newport, Deep Work

Frameworks We Cover

How to get into the work that matters, and how to close it out cleanly at the end of the day.

1

The 4 Rules of Deep Work

From Cal Newport's Deep Work: the ability to focus without distraction on cognitively demanding work is increasingly rare and increasingly valuable — and it's a trained skill, not a personality trait.

1

Work Deeply

Build routines and rituals around your focus sessions instead of relying on willpower each time.

2

Embrace Boredom

Train your attention span deliberately — the same way constant stimulation erodes it, boredom without a phone rebuilds it.

3

Quit Social Media

Not necessarily forever — evaluate each tool by whether its benefit actually outweighs its cost to your attention.

4

Drain the Shallows

Shallow work isn't the enemy, but it expands to fill the day if you let it. Cap it, don't let it cap you.

2

The Shutdown Ritual

Also from Deep Work: a fixed routine for ending the workday on purpose, so unfinished tasks stop quietly running in the background of your evening. The ritual itself matters less than doing the same one every time — it's the signal, not the specific steps, that lets your brain actually let go.

  • Review — scan today's tasks and calendar for anything still open.
  • Capture — for anything unfinished, write down the next concrete step so it's not relying on memory.
  • Confirm — check tomorrow's calendar so nothing's a surprise.
  • Close — say a fixed phrase to yourself — Newport uses "schedule shutdown, complete" — as a deliberate signal that work is done for the day.

Try It

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, or whatever AI tool you use:

I want to protect time for deep, focused work instead of letting my day fill up with shallow tasks. Ask me about my current calendar and where the interruptions usually come from, then help me design one deep work block for tomorrow using the 4 Rules of Deep Work, plus a short Shutdown Ritual I can use to close out each work day.
