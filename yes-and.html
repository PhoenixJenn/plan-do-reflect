Yes, And

The improv rule for building on an idea instead of shutting it down — say yes to what's there, then add to it.

Try It

A founding rule of improv theater: accept what a scene partner offers ("yes") and build on it ("and") rather than negating it ("no, but"). A scene dies the moment someone blocks an offer instead of building on it — the same is true of a brainstorm, a risky pitch, or a half-formed idea someone brings you in a 1:1. Referenced from Leadership Tenets' Be Optimistic, which is really just this rule applied to a leader's first reaction.

No, But

Leads with the flaw. Technically honest, but it ends the idea's momentum before anyone's had a chance to see where it goes.

Yes, And

Leads with what's usable. Accepts the premise, adds something real to it, and only then — if it's still needed — raises the constraint.

What It's Not

Not uncritical agreement, and not a ban on saying no. It's a rule for the first reaction, not a suspension of judgment forever — real constraints, risks, and hard no's still get raised. They just get raised second, after the idea has actually been built on for a moment, not as the reflex that kills it before it's understood.

Example
1

The offer — someone proposes cutting a feature's scope in half to hit a launch date everyone agreed was fixed.

2

No, but — "We already committed to the full scope with stakeholders." True, and the conversation is now over.

3

Yes, and — "Yes, and if we cut it to the core flow, what would we need to tell stakeholders to make that landable?" Same constraint, still on the table — but the idea got far enough to find out if it actually works.

Watch-out

"Yes, and" every idea and the real no's stop landing — people stop hearing them as genuine constraints and start hearing them as the one time you broke character. It's a rule for the first reaction, not a replacement for judgment.

Try It

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, or whatever AI tool you use:

I want to practice "yes, and." Give me five rough, half-formed ideas one at a time — a product idea, a process change, a proposal from a teammate, whatever mix you want. For each one, make me respond with a genuine "yes, and" that builds on it before I'm allowed to raise any constraint or objection. Push back if my "and" is actually a disguised "but."
